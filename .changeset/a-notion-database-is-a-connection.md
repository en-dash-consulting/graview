A Notion database can be kept in step with an app, declared as a connection the way a Google calendar is (Roadmap f-notion-connection). `connections.<name>` with `"system": "notion"` names one `database` (its 32-character id, or `{ "env": "TASKS_DATABASE" }`) and one `kind`, and `properties` maps a field to a property by its name in Notion (`"stage": "Stage"`), or as `{ "name", "type" }` so the checker judges the type; without `properties`, each field finds the property of its name, ignoring case and punctuation, and the kind's name field is the database's title. A database that holds several data sources — Notion's tables gathered under one name since its 2025-09-03 API, which the transport speaks — says which with `source`, its name in Notion or its id; one of a single source needs none, and a connection that names none of several, or one the database does not hold, is refused with a sentence naming them (`the database Reindata holds 4 data sources — Reindeer Fleet, … — so the connection says which with source`). Auth is an internal integration's secret: `graview serve` and `graview preview` read it from `NOTION_TOKEN` (`<auth.env>_TOKEN`, or the `auth.file` the declaration names), and a host hands it through `runConnections`' `token` (the request carries the declaration, so a host can tell a Notion connection's from a Google one's) and the database's id through the new `database` seam. The secret is never written in the document, the op log, the sync state or an export, and `graview check` refuses one written anywhere in a connection (`connection-secret`).

The transport is `notion({ databaseId, token, … })` on `@graview/core/sync`. A pull asks for the pages edited since the last `last_edited_time` it saw, oldest first, following `next_cursor` to the end, and reads the whole database when it has no cursor and every ten minutes, when a page it had linked and no longer finds — archived, in the trash, moved out — is gone here through `remove-<kind>`. A push makes a page for a record made here, sets the properties of one edited here, and archives the page of one removed here. Title and rich text, number, select and status (an `enum` field, its option matched to Notion's ignoring case and punctuation, so `in-progress` is "In progress"), multi-select (a `list`), date (a `date` field holds the day, a `datetime` field the instant in UTC), checkbox, URL, email and phone go both ways; people are read as their names and never written; formulas, rollups and files, and a relation no edge holds, are left alone and said once (`left alone in Notion: Total (formula: …)`). Words are written as plain text in Notion's 2000-character pieces, a pair of surrogates never split. A version is the page's `last_edited_time` with a hash of its values, so our own write coming back is an echo and somebody's edit in the same minute (Notion keeps the time to the minute) is still news; a field both sides changed is held as the engine holds any. Notion's 429 is waited out as its `Retry-After` says, up to a minute a pass; a refused secret is said by the name it is kept under ("Notion refused NOTION_TOKEN (HTTP 401) …"), and a database the integration cannot see as one to share with it. A real workspace is several tables joined by relations, and they are kept as edges (Roadmap f-notion-several-tables): an app declares one Notion connection per table, each to its own kind, and a key of `properties` may name one of the kind's edges, mapped to a relation property — `"reindeer": "Reindeer Fleet"`, or `{ "name": "Reindeer Fleet", "type": "relation" }` — whose target kind is another Notion connection's; without `properties`, an edge whose name a relation property has is kept when every kind it reaches is. A link made in Notion lands through `connect-<kind>-<edge>` and one taken away through `disconnect-<kind>-<edge>`, credited to Notion; one made or taken away here sets the page's relation, the whole list, and comes back as an echo. A relation's value is the Notion ids of the pages it reaches, so it is compared, echoed, held in conflict (under the connection's `conflicts`, the whole relation at once) and sent as a field is; Notion lists a relation's first 25 pages on a page, and the rest are read through the property's own endpoint. A page reached that no connection has linked yet — its table not read yet — is held in the sync state (`unresolved`) and linked once it is, with nothing sent, so the order the connections run in does not matter: they share one set of links (`sharedLinks()`, a `LinkResolver` over every engine), and `runConnections` gives every connection the same one and, after each pass, has each engine link what it held (`engine.resolveHeld()`) — so a host running `runConnections` gets it with nothing to do, and one making engines with `connection()` passes them one `links`. A relation of one keeps the first page Notion lists and says the rest once. A dual relation is kept on the side whose kind declares the edge; declaring both sides keeps both, and a link made on either side converges with nothing more sent. A read-only connection refuses a link made or taken away here on the edges it keeps, as it refuses an edit.

A `SyncReport` now carries `problem`, what the remote said when it could not be reached, and a served store's line says it: `board: could not reach Notion (notion: …); will try again`.

`scriptedNotion()` is a Notion in memory — retrieve, make and archive a database and the data sources it holds (`source(databaseId, …)` adds another), query a source, make, read, change and archive a page, relation properties to another source with a dual twin kept in step and a relation's pages past 25 read through the property item endpoint, with 401, 404, 400 and `limit(n)`'s 429 as Notion answers them — which core's and ship's tests run against: every property type, a round trip both ways, every page of a 205-page query, our write not echoed, a conflict held, an archive each way, the rate limit, a refused secret, an unshared database, a database of two sources read through the one named and refused when none is, long words, a whole read that loses and duplicates nothing, and two tables joined by a dual relation — a link made in Notion arriving as an edge, one made here written to Notion, a page read later linked, both sides converging with nothing sent, a relation of one, a relation both sides changed held, 30 pages of one relation followed, a read-only connection refusing a link, and two connections linked in one `runConnections` pass whichever runs first. `scripts/verify-notion.mjs` runs the same claims against a real workspace (credentials in `~/.graview-notion-test.env`, never printed; not in `pnpm verify` or CI), making its own database inside a page shared with the integration, adding a second data source to it, making two more tables joined by a dual relation and keeping links both ways, and trashing all three at the end — 28 claims, all held against a live workspace — and `--stub` runs them against the scripted Notion. The graview-sync skill and the core and ship READMEs say how to declare one. The transport and the scripted Notion stay on `@graview/core/sync`, which no page reaches; the checker's judgment of a Notion connection rides with the studio, so every face carries 5,635 bytes more minified (1,686 gzipped), under a raise of its budget (`scripts/budget-raises/a-notion-database-is-a-connection-every-face.json`), and judging a relation kept as an edge 2,449 bytes more (690 gzipped), under a second (`scripts/budget-raises/notion-relations-are-kept-as-edges-every-face.json`); a hosted page's first load stays within its budget.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, the wire and derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. Stored formats — additive: a `SyncState` is format 1 as before and may carry `unresolved`, the relations' pages not linked yet, which a build that predates it drops and reads again on the page's next change; a Notion connection's `cursor` is its own JSON inside it. A `ResourceMapping` may say `edges` (our edge → their name), and `SyncOptions` gains `links`, `connectInbound` and `disconnectInbound`. The declaration and check finding codes — additive: `connections.<name>` may say `"system": "notion"`, which a build that predates it refuses by path, a Notion connection may say `source`, a key of a Notion connection's `properties` may name one of the kind's edges, and a property's `type` may be `relation`, and the checker gains `connection-properties`, `connection-relation`, `connection-relation-target` and `connection-connect` (errors), `connection-read-only` and `connection-disconnect` (warnings) and `connection-secret` (error, said only of a connection that holds a secret). `ConnectionRun.calendarId` is optional now, beside `databaseId`. What Graview Cloud does: keeps each workspace's Notion integration secret and the database's id with its other credentials and hands them to `runConnections` through `token` (answering by `request.declaration.system`) and `database`; says `report.problem` where it says a connection could not reach its calendar; offers `system: "notion"` only on a build that has it; and, for several linked tables, runs every Notion connection of an app through one `runConnections` (or one `sharedLinks()`), so a relation finds the record another connection keeps.
