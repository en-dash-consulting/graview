Any place is drawn for an e-ink screen, with what each region of it does, and an app says which places a device offers as screens (Roadmap f-eink-display-profile, f-render-tap-map, f-device-nav-bar). `@graview/pages/device` is a new entry: `renderScreen(store, app, place, { profile: "eink", viewport, viewer?, page?, locale?, timeZone?, today?, base? })` draws a place for a reader on a wall at the viewport its host names — 758 × 1024 for a Kindle Paperwhite 3 held upright — and answers `{ html, css, title, found, renderId, page: { index, count }, taps }`. The profile is a fixed layout the framework works out itself: every row a height the profile sets, every line one line cut short by the stylesheet, every row placed at its pixel, so each press is known without a browser and is exactly where it is drawn. It draws in the sixteen grays of its ramp (`EINK_GRAYS`) with no transition, animation, hover, script, font or anything fetched, sets no type under 20 px, and makes every press at least 64 px on each side (`EINK_LEAST_TARGET`). What does not fit goes onto pages of the same size, turned by a press at the foot, the screen saying which page of how many it is. What a place holds is read as the printed page reads it — `renderPlace` and `renderScreen` now share one reading of a place (`describePlace`'s answer over what the seat may see) — and a record offers the acts the derivation puts first that this seat may run with every argument bound, that change something and lose nothing; an act that asks for a typed answer is not offered. The derived home's counts are each a press to their kind's list. Every app draws on it with no markup of its own.

Each region of `taps` does exactly one of `go` (a place's address, or `job:<name>`), `open` (a record's id), `act` (an act and its arguments), `page` or `refresh`, in the viewport's CSS pixels; `tapAt(taps, x, y)` finds the one under a point. `renderId` is the same for the same store state as the seat sees it, declaration, place, seat, profile, viewport and page, and carries what the screen was drawn for and nothing the seat may not see. `resolveTap(store, app, region, { viewer, renderId })` says what a tap does now: the place to draw next, an act done as the seat that tapped it, or a refusal in words. An act is run only when the screen the app draws now for that seat is the one tapped — the same `renderId` — and the act is on it as drawn; against a store that has moved on, it is judged again as the seat now stands and refused, never run.

A declaration's `devices: { screens: [{ place, label }] }` — in a document and in `defineApp` — names the strip along a device's foot, in order, beside Refresh: each screen a press that goes to its place, the one on screen marked (on a record, its kind's list's). `graview check` refuses a screen naming a place the app does not have or the scene (`device-screen-place`), a word longer than 12 characters (`device-screen-label`), two screens for one place (`device-screen-twice`) and more than 4 screens (`device-screens-too-many`), each in words with its fix. With none declared, a device offers the home and the app's jobs for the seat, else the places its bar stands. `@graview/core/devices` is a new entry: `deviceScreensOf`, `screenPlaceOf`, `deviceFindings` and the limits (`MOST_DEVICE_SCREENS`, `LONGEST_SCREEN_LABEL`); the shapes `AppDevices` and `DeviceScreen` are on `@graview/core`. `editDocument` takes `add-device-screen`, `move-device-screen` and `remove-device-screen`, and the studio adds, moves and takes away a screen (`add-screen`, `move-screen`, `remove-screen`), said to the document as those edits. The graview-lists skill says how. A hosted page carries 0.0 KB more up front (49 bytes), and 0.0 KB more when handed a compiled app (36 bytes): the document's `devices` is read loosely and carried, as a page draws no strip.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged. Stored formats — unchanged. The wire — unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes — a document and `defineApp` take a top-level `devices` (screens of a place and a label), which a document before this one never says; `graview check` says four new codes of it (`device-screen-place`, `device-screen-label`, `device-screen-twice`, `device-screens-too-many`); `editDocument` takes three new ops (`add-device-screen`, `move-device-screen`, `remove-device-screen`); nothing that compiled before compiles differently. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged for every app; the studio's own app has three new acts (`add-screen`, `move-screen`, `remove-screen`).
