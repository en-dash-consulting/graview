Text is typed together over the wire: the third phase of collaborative text, with no editor yet. Two pages typing into one field at once both keep their words, letter for letter, over the socket or the poll. `remote.typing(node, field)` hands an editor a field's typing — `splice(at, remove, insert)` a keystroke at a time, `flush()` on a blur or a caret moved, `waiting()` — and loads `./remote-typing.js` with `@graview/core/typing` the first time, so a page carries none of it until somebody types. Each keystroke is shown at once; a run of them (it ends after 400 ms idle, on a caret jump or after a word) goes to the server as one `type-text` claiming where the page last read the log (`rev`). At most one run per field is on its way: what is typed meanwhile is held, and sent once the server has answered the one before, so the server only carries it over other people's typing. While runs wait the server's ops land under them, and `Store.rebase` carries each pending keystroke over the splices they took on its field (`@graview/core/typing`'s hook): the server's own op for a run is that run, and a run refused is taken out from under the typing after it. A reader that never said it reads op format 3 is sent every splice as a `patch-node` of the field's whole text before and after it (`opsFor(opFormat, ops, store)`) — at a broadcast and in a catch-up alike, and through the seq it is served for a reader of an app that publishes — and converges; a reader that says 3, as `openRemote` does, is sent splices. The server compares no revision of a field typed together as stale, and hands a call's `base` to `store.applyAll`, so a whole write over the wire — an editor's, an agent's — merges three ways with what was typed since; `openRemote` claims where it read the log for such a field. A seat whose op is withheld for another reason is still served its splices on the field it sees, under a `withheld:<hex>` batch. A `type-text` whose `rev` is behind the horizon is a conflict (`theirs` the text now, `saw` the `rev`), which a page offers as theirs or yours whole — `useMine` puts the text it showed over theirs as one keystroke; and a compaction keeps the horizon behind every open socket: `protocol.floor(peers)`, `handler.pins()`, and the adapter handler's and `serveStore`'s `compact(options)`, which passes them as `CompactOptions.pins`. Ship's tests hold that two clients typing at once converge character for character, that an old reader converges by whole texts, that a whole write merges with concurrent typing, that a slow client's held run lands carried over what others typed, that a seat whose op is withheld still converges on the field it sees, that a page back after the horizon moved takes the state and is offered its typing as a conflict, that a keystroke behind the horizon is a conflict, and that a reader served behind the head is sent splices only through its served seq, whole when it did not say format 3; core's, that pending typing is carried over what lands under it and out from under a refused run. A hosted page carries 2.6 KB more up front (in thirteen chunks, as before: the door is tied to `@graview/core`), and under 0.1 KB more on the pages face alone and a visitor's form alone (the rebase's hook), under raises of those budgets (`scripts/budget-raises/text-is-typed-together-over-the-wire-*.json`), and the scene face's limit before it draws moves to 995 KB; Cloud's worker measures 2_067_300 B.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged: no primitive or op field is added; `type-text`'s `rev` may now name any seq its sender had read the log through, not only the field's revision, which reads back the same text. The wire — additive within protocol 1: a reader before op format 3 is no longer sent a `splice-text` it would refuse, but the whole text as a `patch-node`; a call's `base` may name a field typed together, which a server of this build merges and never refuses as stale, and which a server before it compares as it compared any field — so a whole write from this client over a field somebody typed into meanwhile may be refused there as stale where the phase 2 client's was not; a `type-text` with a `rev` behind the horizon is answered `conflict` rather than refused `invalid`; `WIRE_PROTOCOL` does not move. `LiveProtocol` has `floor(peers)`, a host's own implementation of it has one more method to write; `StoreHandler` has `pins()`, and the adapter handler and `serveStore` have `compact`. Snapshot and log formats, the declaration and check finding codes, derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: takes this build; a host that holds its sockets itself passes `protocol.floor(sockets)` among `CompactOptions.pins` when it compacts, and its worker's import budget takes the 7.6 KB this adds there (2_059_703 → 2_067_300 B).
