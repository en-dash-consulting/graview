Letting go of a pan on the one face, nothing moves. At Nearby and the City every board, district name and line moved with the ground while a person panned, and when the pan stopped they all snapped back to where the pan began, then slid over to where they had been. The boards and names are placed in the picture's pixels as the city was last read; a pan written into the view lays the city out again where the hand left it, and the old numbers, drawn against the new picture, stood where the pan began, and the settle after the city was read again started from there. The city read again now says how its districts moved (`carriedBetween`: the pan, and a zoom's growth), what was drawn is carried by the same before anything settles, and a pan that changed nothing but where the city stands against the window places nothing again: what stood stays where the last frame drew it, one the pan brought under the bar, the page or the control is put away until it comes out again, and only a board or name with no place yet is placed, round them (`placeSigns` takes `held`). A drag begun on a board and a scroll over a district, which pan through the view rather than the Scene's hand, no longer trail the ground and slide after it. A zoom let go of places the boards and names again for the new zoom, as before, and each now goes there from where the last frame of the zoom drew it rather than jumping back to where the zoom began. Held by two new claims in `one-face` — `letGoOfAPanEveryBoardAndNameStaysWhereTheLastFrameDrewIt` (a drag on the ground, a drag begun on a board, a scroll and a scroll over a district at a desk, a finger's drag on a phone, at Nearby and the City: no box on the window moves more than a pixel for 600 ms) and `letGoOfAZoomEveryBoardAndNameGoesOnFromWhereTheLastFrameDrewIt` (ctrl and the wheel, two fingers apart) — and by a unit test (`a-pan-let-go-of-carries-what-was-drawn-with-the-ground`). Nothing a host calls changes.
