A template reads what Graview Cloud's templates now say, and a field keeps a time of day (HQ feature f-template-format-cloud-additions). A question may be a `time`, whose answer "3 PM" or "3:30pm" is kept as "15:00"; setup words may say `${id}` where a letter follows the answer (`"${date}T${dinnerTime}:00"`), beside `$id`, which is still the answer itself; a question's `ask` may say an earlier answer as `${id}` (`questionAsked` reads it in); and a template may come with views — `views`, curated worker views, each its manifest (`name`, `attach`, `cardinality`, `title` and the rest of a worker view's manifest), beside `frameViews`, each `name`, `kind`, `cardinality`, `title` and `reads` — their code kept beside the template as `views/<template id>/<name>.js` or `.html`. `graview create --template` writes each view it finds there to the project's `views/`, a curated view's manifest beside it, and says which it left out; `graview apply --template` takes time answers and `${id}` words and names the views it leaves to the host. `graview check template.json` now checks the template as well as its document (`templateFindings`, on `@graview/core/check`): an `$id` or `${id}` no question asks, a question that names one not asked before it, and a view naming a kind, a relation or an act the document lacks are refused, with the path to fix them at.

A field may be `{ "type": "time" }` (`timeField()` in code): a time of day with no day and no zone, kept as `HH:MM`. It refuses anything else, sorts as its hours do, is said "3:00 PM" (in en-US) by a card, a record's facts, a list's CSV and `{x | time}`, is typed in a time input on Pages, in a visitor's form, in place and in the workbench (`control: "time"`, `ArgShape` `{ type: "time" }`), is read from "3 PM" in a form, a spreadsheet cell or a migration (`parseTimeOfDay`, on `@graview/core/words`), and tells an agent its pattern and `x-graview-type: "time"`.

A host can ask whether a seat may run an act on a record without previewing it (HQ feature f-may-seat-run-act-on-record): `mayRun(store, seat, { act, record?, args? })` and `mayRunEach(store, seat, asks)`, on `@graview/core/seat`, answer `{ answer: "allowed" }`, `{ answer: "fill", toFill }` with the arguments still to give by name, or `{ answer: "refused", rule, reason, message, wouldNeed? }`, where `rule` is `policy`, `own-records` (a derived remove, archive, bring-back or merge reaching only what the seat made), `sight`, `condition` (an act's `allowedWhen`, or its own `ActRefusal`), `arguments` or `act` (none by that name, or one that went wrong). Handed a whole call it asks `store.wouldApply(call, seat, judge)`: `apply` now judges each call through one path and writes after it, and the graph judges each write before making it (`Graph.judged`), so the answer is what applying would say for every seat, the app's own actor included; publishing, a vote and a proposal are judged by making their op as `apply` makes it and keeping none, and where a kind follows another (`derives`) what follows the change is worked out as `apply` works it out, over a copy of the graph with the change made. Handed part of a call it judges who may, what the seat may see, the arguments given as the act's input reads them (its defaults filled), an act that writes only fields the seat is not shown, what the app's own actor is held to, and a document act's condition where it reads no argument the caller left out (one optional or with a default included) (`condition` on the act, its expression and the judgment its `apply` throws on), and otherwise says what is still to fill. It writes nothing and copies no store, but for a seat whose profile's person is not made yet: that seat is judged, as `apply` judges it, as the person made first, on the one copy the store keeps for it while it stands (`store.judgedAs(seat)`). A hundred acts on rota are answered in about a millisecond. A refusal by the own-records rule says so (`Refusal.onlyWhatYouMade`).

A hosted page carries 2.8 KB more up front, and one handed a compiled app 2.8 KB more, under raises of their budgets: the time field's schema, input and words, a document act's condition handed as data and apply's one judging path. The pages face, a visitor's form, every face, the embed and Cloud's worker grow with it, under raises of their budgets.

Tests: `a time of day is a field of its own`, `a template asks a time and comes with views` (core), `a template sets up a live store` (tools), `a seat is told what it may run without running it`, `a seat is told to fill what a code act and a new profile need`.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged, though an act may now refuse a call it used to take, and a document act's argument left out is its default to its condition and effects. Stored formats — a record may hold a time field's `HH:MM` string; nothing else stored changes. The wire — unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes — a new field type, `time`; a template may carry `views` and `frameViews`, a `time` question and `${id}` in setup words and asks; `graview check` of a template adds the codes `question` (an ask naming an id) and `view` beside `setup`. Derived tool names and input schemas — a time field's argument says `x-graview-type: "time"` and its `HH:MM` pattern; every other schema is unchanged.
