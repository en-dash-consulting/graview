Calendar events invite the people they are about, Roadmap f-calendar-guests-mapping and f-calendar-guests-recipe. A Google calendar connection kept an event's title, its times and its place, and never who was coming: a household's dinner linked to the two people it is for reached the calendar with no guests, and an invitation sent from the calendar reached nobody here. Now a connection's `fields` may name `guests` — a field of the kind holding the addresses (a `list`, or one `email`), kept as the attendees; or `{ "via": "who" }`, the kind's relation to the people, each reached person's `emails` invited while their `inviteMe` is on (`emails` and `invite` name other fields; `invite: false` says the app has no toggle and every address is invited). The relation rides the engine's edge machinery: a person's remote id is their GUEST KEY (their addresses lowercased, once each, sorted and joined with ","), so a person with two addresses is one guest with two attendees, an attendee whose address is a person's links that person through `connect-<kind>-<relation>`, and one matching nobody — the calendar's organizer, a stranger — is held as part of the relation's value (`SyncState.unresolved`) and sent back as it was, never dropped; turning Invite me on links what was held, with nothing sent for it. A person without an address or with the toggle off is no guest either way: never sent, never matched. A field of addresses is compared as the calendar keeps it (`ResourceMapping.normalize`), so "Ben@Example.com " typed here is not sent on every pass. The transport says `sendUpdates` on every insert, patch and delete — `all` with `notify: true` on `guests`, `none` otherwise — where Google used to decide, so nobody is mailed unless the declaration asks. `graview check` judges it in a person's words: `connection-guests-field` (the field, or the reached kind's `emails` or `invite`, missing or of the wrong type, with the `add-field` that fixes it as a `fixes` entry a host applies in one click) and `connection-guests-via` (not a relation of the kind, one reaching several kinds, or "who reaches person, and a person has no emails to invite"; a warning naming the app's people kind when the relation reaches another). The `graview-sync` skill carries the recipe for "invite my email on events I'm on" (`calendar-events-invite-the-people-they-are-about.test.ts`; against Google itself, `verify-google-calendar.mjs` proves two emails are two attendees, taken off when unlinked, an attendee added there linking its person, and a stranger kept through a round trip). A hosted page's first load is unchanged: the mapping is the connection's, run by a host. Cloud's worker, which runs connections and checks documents, carries 10_332 bytes more; every face and an embed handed the studio, whose editor judges a connection, carry 3_683 bytes more minified and 1_123 / 1_111 gzipped (the checker's judgment of `guests`); each under a raise of its own.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, the wire, and derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes: additive — a Google calendar connection's `fields` takes `guests`, which a build that predates it refuses by path; `connection-guests-field` and `connection-guests-via` are new codes, and no declaration that passed is refused. Stored formats: `SyncState` is unchanged in shape; a guests relation's held ids are addresses rather than Notion page ids, and `RemoteChange.fields` from the calendar carries `attendees` (an empty list for an event with none). What Graview Cloud does: offer `guests` where it draws a calendar connection's fields, with the people kind's relations to pick `via` from, and apply each finding's `fixes` as it does `connection-creates`'s.
