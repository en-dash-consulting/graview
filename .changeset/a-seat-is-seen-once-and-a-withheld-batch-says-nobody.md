**Security.** A withheld op's batch says nobody's session, and a seat is seen once per publish. A withheld op kept the batch its author's client minted, `batch:<that browser's tag>:<n>`, so a seat that may not see a change could still tell which session made it, and link it to the changes it may see. Now every route and the live socket serve a withheld op under an opaque batch, `withheld:<16 hex>` — a keyed SHA-256 of the batch, the same for every op of one batch, and not one a seat can work back from — in `ops`, an ack, a welcome's state, `/graview/state`, `/graview/since`, `/graview/here` and `/graview/export`; the seat that made the change is served its own batch, and an op the seat may see keeps its own. The key is drawn once per store held, so every route and socket over one store agree; `liveProtocol({ withheldKey })` gives a host's own secret, for the same opaque batch across wakes. And `publish` redacted the ops once per socket: two hundred ops to fifty sockets took 40–46 ms in Graview Cloud's spike, though fifty sockets are a handful of seats. Now `publish` and `tell` make each seat's view once — a seat key resolved once, each run of ops redacted and written once — and send it down every socket that seat holds: the same two hundred ops to fifty sockets of five seats take 4.7 ms where they took 43, and to fifty sockets of one seat 1.0 ms where they took 46 (median of seven, this machine); fifty sockets of fifty seats are still fifty views, at 43 ms. The FR-55 property tests hold: no unseen id leaves the wire over 1,000 random worlds, on the socket and over HTTP.

Compatibility: the wire and live protocols — narrowing, no field added or removed, `WIRE_PROTOCOL` unchanged. A seat with sights is now sent withheld ops of other people under `withheld:<16 hex>` batches where it was sent their own; nothing can undo a withheld op, so no client named those batches. `LiveProtocolOptions` gains `withheldKey`. `REFUSAL_REASONS`, ops, stored formats and check codes are unchanged.
