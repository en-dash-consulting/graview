A lighter composer for a host's pages. Graview Cloud lends `composeChange` to its owner and editor pages, so the quick ask, "Make “Tasks” better" and "Other ways to show this" can offer a change to the app rehearsed before it is offered. `composeAppChange` from `@graview/core/compose` carries the whole composer and the checker: fetched from Cloud's shell when asked for, it was 565_839 bytes (193_883 gzipped) behind its door and took 77_398 bytes (22_651 gzipped) of zod and of the brand's rules into every visitor's first load, because a bundler places a module in one chunk with every part of it any chunk uses. Cloud bundled it apart instead, about 660 KB with a second copy of core, and the shell's store did not recognize that copy's errors when it rehearsed a plan.

`@graview/core/composer` is the part a page needs. Its `composeAppChange` applies the plan's edits, grants what they add as the app's widest grant does and says the same steps as the checked composer, then compiles the changed document with the compile a page already has (`compileDocumentWithoutCheck`): the document's own sentences are judged, view specs and rules among them, and the checker is left to the host, whose `onProposeChange` judges the change again before it keeps it. It composes edits only; a reshape's `add` and `drop` are refused in words and stay with `@graview/core/compose`. Fetched from Cloud's shell handed a compiled app, it is 273_193 bytes (92_888 gzipped) behind its door, with no checker, no reshape traits and no zod in it, and adds 8 KB to what every visitor loads first. That is because `editDocument`'s edit shapes are now written in zod/mini, as the document's own shapes are; the edits take and refuse exactly what they did. The page that compiles its document in the browser still carries `editDocument` up front through `@graview/core/document` once any door uses it, so a host that lends the composer is best handed a compiled app.

And a store recognizes the errors of another copy of core. `SchemaError`, `GraphError`, `MissingRecordError`, `PermissionDeniedError`, `ActRefusal` and `UndoBlockedError` each carry a registered symbol and answer `instanceof` for an error from any copy on the page, so a record of a kind another copy's schema lacks is refused with that schema's own error rather than read as a field that did not fit, and `refusalOf` reads another copy's refusal by its reason. A subclass is still told apart from its parent. Held by unit tests of core (`a-page-composes-a-change-with-the-compile-it-already-has`, `a-store-recognizes-the-errors-of-another-copy-of-core`) and by `tests/the-composer-a-hosted-page-fetches-keeps-to-its-budget`, which builds the page as Cloud builds it and holds the door to 285 KB minified and 97 KB gzipped and the first load to 10 KB more. The marks add 0.5 KB up front to a hosted page (535 / 183 bytes handed a compiled app, 553 / 138 compiling), and about the same to the pages face, a visitor's form and the guest host.

Compatibility: the declaration and check finding codes are unchanged: `editDocument` takes and refuses the same edits with the same sentences, and `composeAppChange` from `@graview/core/compose` answers as before. The wire is unchanged: a refusal carries the same reason and sentence. Ops and primitives, stored formats, and derived tool names and input schemas are unchanged. The JavaScript API is additive: `@graview/core/composer` with `composeAppChange`, and `instanceof` for the store's errors now answers for another copy's. What Graview Cloud does: lend `composeAppChange` from `@graview/core/composer`, fetched from the shell, in place of its separate composer bundle, and keep judging a kept change with the checker in `onProposeChange`.
