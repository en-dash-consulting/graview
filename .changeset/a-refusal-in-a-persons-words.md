A refusal on the wire is said to a person. The live wire and `POST /graview/ops` sent the store's own sentences, written for a developer — `Invalid arguments for mutation "add"` with each argument's key, and `Unknown mutation "dance"` followed by every act the app has, listed to any seat that sent a name it does not have. Now the default says arguments that do not fit field by field in the words the form asked in ("“Add a task” was not made: Name — too small: …"), and an act the app does not have by the name the call gave and no other ("This app has no act called “dance”."); every other refusal keeps its own sentence, the policy's among them. `wireRefusalOf(store, error)` is that wording, exported from `@graview/ship` and `@graview/ship/runtime`. And `liveProtocol({ refusal: (error, calls, peer) => … })` lets a host word a refusal itself — Graview Cloud says its own quarantine and quota sentences — on the socket and through `post`: it is handed what the store threw, the refused calls and the socket with its seat resolved, and answers the refusal to send, or nothing for ship's. In `@graview/core`, an unknown act now throws `UnknownMutationError` (a `GraphError`, with the same message, and `mutation`, the name the call gave), so a surface can tell it apart without reading the message.

Compatibility: the wire and live protocols — a refusal's `sentence` changes for invalid arguments and an unknown act, and nothing else; its `reason` is unchanged (`invalid`), and no field is added or removed. A program that matched on `Invalid arguments for mutation` or `Unknown mutation` in a wire sentence should branch on `reason`, as FR-46 meant it to. `LiveProtocolOptions` gains `refusal`. In core, `UnknownMutationError` is a new export, and `store.mutation(name)` throws it where it threw a plain `GraphError` with the same message: a `catch` of `GraphError` still catches it. `WIRE_PROTOCOL`, `REFUSAL_REASONS`, ops, stored formats and check codes are unchanged.
