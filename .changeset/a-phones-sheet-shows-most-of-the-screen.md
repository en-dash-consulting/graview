A phone's sheet shows most of the screen (Roadmap feature:f-scene-sheet-keeps-the-city, Nick's follow-up: "it could also take up some more screen space. looks like it goes halfway up"). The sheet a chosen district, a record or a picture opens on a phone stood at half the picture (a picture's at 60%), and a person reading a district's records saw a handful of rows under a half-empty map. It opens at two thirds now, with the chosen district still lifted above it — the city stays where it stood — and a handle at its top takes it up to nearly all of the picture or down to the half it was: dragged, it comes to rest at the nearest of the three; pressed, it steps up one; and it is a separator the keyboard reaches, its arrows, Home and End stepping between the rests, saying to a screen reader how much of the screen it takes ("Two thirds of the screen"). While a finger moves it only the sheet follows; the map is laid out again once, when it comes to rest. A sheet opens at the rest the last one was left at, for the rest of the visit.

Taken up nearly to the top, the sheet leaves a strip of map no district stands in whole: the choice is not pushed into it, under the scene's own controls at the top, and no signs are drawn at the edges of a strip too thin to point from, where the sign for what lay past its top stood on the one for what lay past its foot.
