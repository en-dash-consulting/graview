A grant can hold to your own end of a link (the Graview Roadmap's f-own-end-grants). `self` holds a grant to the act's subject being the principal's own record, and the act that takes back a vote is on the idea, so a voter could take back anyone's. `ownEnd: true` on a grant holds a connect or sever to every record the call names at the far end of the link being the principal's own. The store refuses another with a sentence — "Not permitted: “Take back a vote” on an idea — only on your own end of the link." and the grant's own words — the seat keeps the tool, and the strip offers the act on an idea you voted for with you already chosen, and not at all on one only somebody else voted for. `graview check` refuses the condition on an act that connects and severs nothing, or names no record at its far end (`grant-own-end-without-link`), and asks for a people kind (`people-unsaid`).

Compatibility: additive. The document format gains `policy.grants[].ownEnd` within `graview-document@1`; a build that predates it refuses it by path. Check finding codes: `grant-own-end-without-link` is new, an error only about a grant that could not be written before. A refusal's `reason` is `forbidden`, as any grant's. Tool names and schemas are unchanged; the affordances of an act refused for want of a far end now narrow to the seat's own as they did for a subject. Ops, stored formats and the wire are unchanged. New: `Grant.ownEnd`, and `permits`'s eighth argument, the records the call names at the far end of what it links.
