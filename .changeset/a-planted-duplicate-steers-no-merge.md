A duplicate somebody plants beside another person's record no longer steers the app's own actor into merging theirs away. The duplicates judge kept the record with the most links and merged the other into it, so a member who may add vendors and tasks, and may not merge or archive the owner's vendor, could add a vendor of the same name, link two tasks of their own to it, and have the actor — a system author no grant narrows — propose merging the owner's vendor into theirs at 0.9; with `applyAtLeast` at 0.9 it applied it, archiving the owner's vendor and moving the owner's task to the member's. Now the record that stays is the one made first, as the log says (a record from before the log, a seed's, before any it made), and only among records made at one moment the one with the most links: nobody can make a record earlier than one already there. A merge of two records different people made is suggested for a person to accept, never applied on its own, whatever `applyAtLeast` says; two records one person made merge on their own as before. And where the two have one name, the merge's sentence says which is which by id — "Merge Northwind Parts (vendor:northwind-parts) into Northwind Parts (vendor:owner)" — where it read the same both ways. `a-planted-duplicate-steers-no-merge` holds it: the planted vendor is the one proposed to merge away, nothing is applied with `applyAtLeast` 0.9 and the owner's task keeps its vendor, the sentence names the direction, and two vendors one person made are merged on their own.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, the declaration and check finding codes — unchanged. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged; the derived `merge-<kind>`'s sentence names each record's id beside its name when the two names are the same. `JudgeContext` may carry `made` (when and by whom each record was made, from the log), and `keeperOf` takes it. What Graview Cloud does: nothing; a merge the actor used to apply across two people's records now waits in the tray.
