A frame guest holds back the page's drawn signal until it has drawn (FR-188). The embed's root said `data-graview-drawn` once every worker view on the place had drawn or failed, but a frame guest (`guestView({ url })`, the sandboxed iframe) was not counted, so a place drawn by one was pictured with its frame still empty. Now the frame says `data-guest-view-drawn` once the guest has said it is ready, been handed what it shows (and the pictures it asked for) and asked for its height — what the frame protocol says once it has rendered — or stayed quiet for 250 ms after, for a guest that never asks one; and `data-guest-view-failed="silent"` when it does not say it is ready in `limits.readyMs` (8 s by default, a new limit of `mountGuestView`), so a frame that never loads counts as settled rather than holding the page to its ten seconds. A guest that says it is ready after all is drawn then. The frame is `aria-busy` until one or the other, and `guestView`'s holder is until the frame's host has drawn the frame in it, which is what the embed already waits for. A hosted page carries 0.0 KB (28 bytes) more up front for it (the compiled page too), and their budgets are raised by that; the guest host before a worker is drawn and every face by what the frame's host now measures.

Compatibility: ops, stored formats, the wire, the declaration and derived tools are unchanged; the frame protocol says nothing new. New: `limits.readyMs` on `mountGuestView` and `guestView`; the frame's `data-guest-view-drawn`, `data-guest-view-failed="silent"` and `aria-busy`, and `aria-busy` on `guestView`'s holder until its frame is drawn. What Graview Cloud does: nothing more — `[data-graview-drawn]` now waits for a place's frame guests too.
