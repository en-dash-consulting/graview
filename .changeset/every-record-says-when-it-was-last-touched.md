Every record says when it was made and last touched, and by whom, kept by the framework. A view's nodes carried no created or updated time and no last editor, no expression could read a record's last change, and an app on Graview Cloud kept a `lastTouched` date of its own, set by fifteen acts, which missed files, links, imports and tidying and read in UTC. Now every record keeps `createdAt`, `createdBy`, `updatedAt` and `updatedBy`. Every op that touches a record stamps `updatedAt` and `updatedBy` on it as it lands — a field, a file, a link at either end, an import, a host's own ops, the app's own tidying, an undo — and the op that adds one stamps all four; in the store, an undo of a removal puts the record back with when it was first made, and a document migration that changes nothing of a record touches nothing (a kind renamed keeps when each was made). `…At` is the op's time; `…By` is who acted as `$me` says it: the record of the people kind the seat is bound to, else the account's id (an agent acting for a person is that person; a profile is the account's and the profile's, `~` between; a seat with no id is its kind). No act or patch writes them.

They are the record's own, so a snapshot, a checkpoint, the archive and an export carry them, and they are read wherever a field is: a view's nodes, `get_node` and `find`, a seat's view (judged by sight like any field: a sight may `hide: ["updatedBy"]` from the records a seat is served, though its log still names who made each change it may see), a list (sorted, filtered and grouped by "Last changed" and "Added", by day on the app's clock: `?sort=updatedAt:desc`, `filter=updatedAt:on:2026-11-20`), a template (`{updatedAt | relative}`, `{updatedAt | date}`, in the app's `brand.timeZone`), and the rule language (rules, computed fields, a job's `where` and `sort`). A seat is told them by the log it is served: an op served withheld says when, never who; what a removed record's payload carried is not served, so a record a seat sees put back or come into its sight is made, to that seat, then; and of the checkpoint a compacted log begins at, a seat is told the times alone. A record's own page and card say its fields and not the four, unless its glance or page asks for them. A store from before them opens with what its log holds: a record takes them from the ops that wrote it, its making only from the op that added it, and says none rather than a time made up. `graview check` refuses a kind that declares a field, relation or computed value of one of these names (`touched-field-taken`). On `@graview/core`: `CREATED_AT`, `CREATED_BY`, `UPDATED_AT`, `UPDATED_BY`, `TOUCHED_FIELDS`, `isTouchedField`, `touchedOf`, `toucherOf`, `untouched` (a record without the four) and `Touched`, and `Graph.touch`. The `graview-view-specs` and `graview-lists` skills say how to read them. Held by a unit test of core (`every-record-says-when-it-was-made-and-last-touched-and-by-whom`).

Compatibility: stored formats — additive: a record holds the four as keys of its own, no format number moves, and a store stored before them opens with what its log still holds and no time made up; `snapshotHash` leaves them out, so a graph hashes as it did. Ops and primitives — unchanged: no op carries them, each fold stamps them from the op's `at` and author, and a patch naming one is passed over. The wire — additive: records carry them, and a seat's are its served log's own. The declaration and check finding codes — breaking for a declaration with a kind that declares a field, relation or computed value named `createdAt`, `createdBy`, `updatedAt` or `updatedBy` (`touched-field-taken`); every other declaration checks as before, and a rule, template, view or job may name the four. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: rename any app field of those names before it takes this version (an app's own `lastTouched` field and the acts that set it can go), read the four off a record wherever it showed its own, and say `…By` through its directory, since it is a seat's id or a person's record id.
