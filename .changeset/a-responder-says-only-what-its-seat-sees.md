A responder handed the whole store says only what its seat sees. The chat hands its responder the provider's store, already narrowed to the seat, but `llmResponder` and `seatResponder` called directly with the store itself wrote the whole graph into the model's system prompt: every kind's names, the links between them, the broken rules, the selection and the graph's first reading, whoever was asking, while the turn's tools stayed sighted. The prompt is now written over `store.seenBy(context.principal)` whatever store it is handed: a record the seat may not see is not said by name or id, in the shape, the connections, the rules, the selection or the reading, and a model's answer is resolved by name and validated over the same graph. `seatResponder`'s graph answer is read as that seat too. With no principal, a responder does not read the whole graph: its prompt, its turn's tools and the reading of its answer are all as `{ kind: "agent" }`, the seat a tool runtime acts as when no author is given, and where the policy keeps anything from that seat the answer says so: "(No seat was named, so this was read as any agent may see it.)". A store that keeps nothing from anybody is read as before, and says nothing extra. Nothing a page loads up front changed: the prompt is the seat's model's, fetched with the first ask a model answers.

Compatibility: ops, stored formats, wire messages, the document format, the compiled format, check finding codes and tool names and schemas are unchanged. Changed: `llmResponder` and `seatResponder` read as `context.principal`, and without one as `{ kind: "agent" }`. What a host does: one that calls a responder itself names the seat it answers for in `principal`; one that relied on a responder with no principal reading the whole graph names a seat that may see it.
