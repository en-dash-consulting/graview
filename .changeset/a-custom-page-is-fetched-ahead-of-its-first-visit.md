A page drawn by a view of the app's own comes sooner on its first visit. Where a pointer rests on a link or one of the bar's places, a finger or a pointer goes down on it, or the keyboard comes to it, the view's code, the host's halves that draw it and what it keeps for its viewer (`viewerState.read`) are fetched as the visit would fetch them, so the page waits only for its worker to start and draw; and once the page is idle, the code alone of the first four of the app's places. Each view once, at most 24 a page; none for a seat that sees nothing the view reads or is not offered the place; none where the browser says to save data or the connection is 2G. No worker is started, no record read and no act asked ahead. What a view keeps for its viewer is asked of the host once for every drawing of it that wants it at the same moment, and once for a visit asked ahead; it is forgotten as the seat, the store, the registry or being signed in changes; and a view gone back to asks again, and is pushed only what moved. A frame guest's host half is fetched ahead, its document never.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, derived tool names and input schemas, and the declaration and check finding codes — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: nothing to call. `worker` functions and `viewerState.read` may be asked up to some ten seconds before the page that uses them, and once for a visit that is then not made; both must answer for the seat the page is signed in as, as they do now. Keep `viewerState` one object across navigations.
