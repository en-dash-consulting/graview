A routed page can step back into the app's city and come in again as it was (Graview HQ feature:f-page-zooms-out-to-the-city). The bar of a face that owns its window holds one more control, a toggle named "Step back to the city". Pressed, the app's city becomes the ground under the bar, and the page stands in front of it as one plane: the same elements, nothing mounted again, at half its size (a little more on a phone), up and to the left, and turned on its left edge in perspective so it reads as a panel standing in the city (20 degrees at a desk, 9 on a phone). The city is the scene's own map, from the scene's own arithmetic: each kind the seat may see on its plot of `cityMap`, walked in the order a blank installation fills its kinds, a row of blocks per value of its main choice with what is done lying flat, in the kind's hue and the scene's lighting, and each relation down the streets between districts as the scene routes it. Its districts are drawn down and to the right of the page's corner, so most of them are not under the page. A thin line in the record's hue runs from each part of the page that shows a record (a relation's link, a listed row, any link to a record's page) to that record's building, and one runs from the page's title to the record the page is. Each line starts where the page's turned plane puts that part, carried through the page's own transform, and ends in a ring around the roof, drawn over the page too, so a record the page stands in front of is still found. Each record a line runs to is a stop for the keyboard, named for its record, that opens its page. What the lines join is said once for a screen reader. In a page that reads right to left everything is mirrored: up and to the right, turned on its right edge, the city down and to the left. Pressed again, Escape, or a press on the page brings it back in (from a way into the city, the keyboard waits on the control): the scroll it was at, the keyboard where it was on the page, and the address unchanged. Stepped back from a scrolled window, the page shows exactly what was on screen. Going somewhere while stepped back opens the new page in. With reduced motion asked for there is no travel either way and no fade of the city, only where it ends. The stepped-back page takes no pointer and no keyboard (`inert`); the keyboard, if it was on the page, waits on the control.

The city is fetched the first time a person steps back, so a page that is only read draws as it did. Its streets are routed between frames and remembered for the ground they were routed on, the way the map runs is chosen from its plots alone, and the map and its blocks are kept for the store's version across steps (and let go with the store), so stepping back on twenty-five districts holds no frame and a second step back routes nothing. The page is DOM over the city, turned with a CSS transform. Where a browser can draw a live element into a canvas, the one sheet that steps back is the element a city drawing it in would take; that capture stays the render package's to make, and this face still reaches none of it. An embedded face, whose page is its host's, is not offered the control, nor is a design that owns the whole window without the bar. Whether the city stands behind the page or beside it is one switch (`CITY_PLACEMENT`). `@graview/react/ground` is a new entry: `ribbonsOf`, `ribbonWidth`, `routeOnTheGround`, `routeBetweenFrames` and `routesRemembered`, the scene's streets and their router, for a picture of the city drawn elsewhere. It is apart from the main entry, so the router rides into no face that does not ask for it. Routes asked for a picture of the city drawn off the scene say so (`elsewhere`), and the scene's camera never draws its streets from them.

A hosted page carries 0.0 KB more up front (37 bytes, the glue of one more chunk, whose door is tied to what the page drew first so the page still loads thirteen chunks before it draws), under a raise of both its budgets. The pages face alone grows by 6.6 KB (2.6 KB gzipped), and every face by 19.4 KB (8.6 KB gzipped) with the city's own chunk, under raises of their budgets.

Tests: `the-scene-never-draws-streets-routed-for-a-picture-elsewhere` (react) holds that the scene's camera draws no street routed for a picture elsewhere. `a-page-steps-back-over-the-city-and-comes-in-as-it-was` (pages) holds the geometry (the page at half its size, up and to the left, turned less on a phone, the city behind it in the room past the page's corner, a line leaving a part from a point the page's plane puts it at) and that the bar's toggle takes a record's page out, inert and turned, Escape brings it in, a node and a half-typed field are the same out and back in, a page that reads right to left is mirrored, a second press before it moves keeps it in, and an embedded face has none. `the-city-behind-a-page-holds-no-frame-on-a-big-app` (pages) draws twenty-five districts of 32 and of 200 records each under 120 ms with no call into the router while it draws, draws it again for the same store in under 10 ms, routes its streets once between frames, and answers a second step back and a change that moves no plot from what it routed. `pnpm verify step-back` drives the todo example and the garden at a desk and on a phone, in Chromium and WebKit: every routed page has the control, the keyboard reaches it, out the page stands angled in front of the city, the city fills the ground behind it, a record's page draws a line from its title and from each part that links to a record, each starting on that part as the turned page draws it, the keyboard reaches those records in the city and opens one, Escape from one leaves the keyboard on the control, back in the scroll, the keyboard and the address are kept, stepped back from a scrolled window the page shows exactly what was on screen, and reduced motion skips the travel.
