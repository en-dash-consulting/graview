A routed page can step back over the app's city and come in again as it was (Graview HQ feature:f-page-zooms-out-to-the-city). The bar of a face that owns its window holds one more control, a toggle named "Step back to the city". Pressed, the page under the bar stays drawn — the same elements, nothing mounted again — and recedes as one plane to 55% of its size, up and to the left, its edge and its shadow over the ground; the app's city stands in the room it leaves, to its right at a desk and under it on a phone, whichever draws the city larger. The city is the scene's own map, from the scene's own arithmetic: each kind the seat may see on its plot of `cityMap`, walked in the order a blank installation fills its kinds, a row of blocks per value of its main choice with what is done lying flat, in the kind's hue and the scene's lighting, and each relation down the streets between districts as the scene routes it. A thin line in the record's hue runs from each part of the page that shows a record — a relation's link, a listed row, any link to a record's page — to that record's building, whose roof is ringed, and one from the page's title to the record the page is; what the lines join is said once for a screen reader. Pressed again, Escape, or a press on the page brings it back in: the scroll it was at, the keyboard where it was on the page, and the address unchanged. Going somewhere while stepped back opens the new page in. With reduced motion asked for there is no travel either way, only where it ends. The stepped-back page takes no pointer and no keyboard (`inert`); the keyboard, if it was on the page, waits on the control.

The city is fetched the first time a person steps back, so a page that is only read draws as it did. The page is DOM over the city with a CSS transform. Where a browser can draw a live element into a canvas, the one sheet that steps back is the element a city drawing it in would take; that capture stays the render package's to make, and this face still reaches none of it. An embedded face, whose page is its host's, is not offered the control, nor is a design that owns the whole window without the bar. `@graview/react/ground` is a new entry: `ribbonsOf`, `ribbonWidth` and `routeOnTheGround`, the scene's streets and their router, for a picture of the city drawn elsewhere — apart from the main entry, so the router rides into no face that does not ask for it.

A hosted page carries 0.0 KB more up front (37 bytes, the glue of one more chunk, whose door is tied to what the page drew first so the page still loads thirteen chunks before it draws), under a raise of both its budgets. The pages face alone grows by 5.3 KB (2.1 KB gzipped), and every face by 14.8 KB (6.7 KB gzipped) with the city's own chunk, under raises of their budgets.

Tests: `a-page-steps-back-over-the-city-and-comes-in-as-it-was` (pages) holds the set-back geometry — the page at 55%, up and to the left, the room to its right and under it, the city in whichever draws it larger, a line leaving a part by its nearest side — and that the bar's toggle takes a record's page out, inert and scaled, Escape brings it in, and an embedded face has none. `pnpm verify step-back` drives the todo example and the garden at a desk and on a phone, in Chromium and WebKit: every routed page has the control, the keyboard reaches it, out the page is smaller and set back with its title in the window and the city clear of it, a record's page draws a line from its title to its own building and one to each record it links to, back in the scroll, the keyboard and the address are kept, and reduced motion skips the travel.

Compatibility: the look — the routed face's bar holds one more control, and a page may step back over the city.
