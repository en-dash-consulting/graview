A store can adopt the server's whole state. `Store.rebase` lands the server's ops on the confirmed prefix a client holds, and after a resync that prefix cannot be trusted: the copy drifted, so an op of the server's no longer fits it, or the server compacted past where the client left off, so `/graview/since` cannot bring the ops between. The client has to take the server's graph and log as they are, and the only way to replace a store's log was to empty its private array, which Graview Cloud's live client did. `store.adopt({ snapshot, log, epochs?, horizon?, pending?, drop? })` is the public way. It reads the `pending` batches off the log as it stands, takes `log` with its epochs and horizon (checked as `OperationLog.from` checks them, so a log that is not intact is refused before anything moves, and a log compacted behind an undo horizon is taken as it is), loads `snapshot` as the graph in the server's order, and applies the pending batches again on top exactly as `rebase` does, saying any that no longer apply in `refused`. Subscribers hear one change, the net diff, with the ops the store did not hold before and the re-applied ones. It returns `{ diff, adopted, pending, refused }`. `OperationLog.replace(ops, epochs?, { horizon? })` is the log's half: another log, wholesale, all or nothing. A store's default op ids now count on past an adopted log as they do past a hydrated one. Ship's `openRemote` never reloads wholesale, so it is unchanged (FR-53).

Compatibility: additive. Ops and primitives unchanged: `OperationLog` gains `replace`, and no op or seq means anything new. Stored formats unchanged. The wire unchanged: `capabilities().shipped` names FR-53. `Store` gains `adopt`; `Adopt` and `AdoptResult` are new types.
