The one face holds on a slower machine, and in another typeface. Run on GitHub's runners for the first time, three of its promises broke where they had held on a laptop:

- A scroll let go of at the City moved a board after the hand stopped. A scroll's last step can reach the picture a frame after the face read the hand's word for it; read there, the move was taken for the city's own and everything was placed afresh, so the "connections" board slid 190 pixels up after the scroll ended. A move read within the let-go time of a hand's pan is now the hand's, and what stands stays where the last frame drew it.
- A district's "+n" was placed round at the size the picture would grow it to, not the size it is drawn. Its type is a size on the screen, so at the City a "+26" read at Nearby was taken to be 16 by 9 pixels where it was drawn 23 by 13, and the page's own board stood against it. It now keeps its size and moves only where its right end goes (`wordOnScreen`).
- A board stood flush against a district's "+n" ("Extinctions+26" on Deep Time's home at 768 wide). A board now stands four pixels clear of one wherever it has a clear place to stand, as a name already did; beside a page, a board is never left standing nowhere for the margin alone.

Held by unit tests of pages (`a-districts-count-keeps-its-size-on-the-screen`, and a board clear of a "+n" in `the-city-says-every-name-and-stands-every-view-clear`) and by the `one-face` harness, which now runs in Nightly with `embed-one-face`.

Compatibility: the JavaScript API, ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, the declaration and check finding codes, and derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: nothing.
