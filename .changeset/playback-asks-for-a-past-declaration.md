Playback asks the host for a past declaration when it reaches one, instead of needing every version handed up front. To draw the past under the declaration then in force, `HistoryHost.declarations` had to carry each version's compiled declaration before the page opened — 27 of them on Cloud's Roadmap app — so a host that sent only the sentences had its past drawn under today's declaration. `HistoryHost.declarationAt(seq)` now answers a mark handed without its `declaration`, called with that mark's `seq` the first time playback reaches a moment under it and asked once per mark (per `declarationAt` function, so hand the same one), returning the declaration or a promise of it. While it is on its way the face holds the picture it had rather than flash the moment under today's declaration, and draws it under today's after 400ms if the answer is slow; an answer of `undefined`, or one that fails, is as if the mark had no declaration — the last one handed before it, else today's. Marks handed with a declaration are never asked about and work as before, and a page that never opens the past asks nothing: the asking lives on `@graview/core/past` as `askDeclaration(host, seq)`, which answers `{ declaration }` or `{ waiting }`, and `pastAt` reads it. It threads wherever `history` is handed — `GraviewProvider`, `mount`/`FrameOptions` and `PagesApp`'s `PageContext`. The holding is a few hooks in the provider's playback, 0.5 KB more up front on a hosted page.

Compatibility: ops, stored formats, the wire, the declaration and derived tools are unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: keep sending the marks with their sentences only, and add `declarationAt: (seq) => fetchVersionDeclaration(seq)` to the `history` it hands, answering with the compiled `{ schema, invariants?, policy?, modules? }` of the version that began at that seq.
