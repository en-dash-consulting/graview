`graview check` knows every act the store answers, so a grant naming `leave-alone-<kind>` or `allow-tidying-<kind>` is no longer refused as one "no mutation declares". The store gives every kind those two acts, and honors a grant naming either; the checker listed the derived acts by hand, and that list had fallen behind. It now reads the store's own list (`deriveMutations`): each kind's edit, remove, archive, bring-back, merge, leave-alone and its undoing, a repeating date's skip, detach and split, and a relation's connect and disconnect, for a grant and a rule's repair alike. A grant may also name `keep-link` and `type-text`, which every store answers and judges by the policy. A grant naming `vote-suggestion`, `propose-changes` or `publish`, which a store answers without a grant (publishing by the roles `publishing.publishers` names), is a warning, `grant-names-ungranted-act`, saying the grant gives nothing. Held by a unit test of core (`check-knows-every-act-the-store-answers`).

Compatibility: the declaration and check finding codes — additive: a grant naming `leave-alone-<kind>`, `allow-tidying-<kind>`, `keep-link` or `type-text` is no longer `grant-unknown-mutation`, nor is a repair naming a leave-alone or allow-tidying act `repair-unknown-mutation`; a grant naming `vote-suggestion`, `propose-changes` or `publish` is the new warning `grant-names-ungranted-act` instead of that error. A declaration that checked clean still checks clean. Ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, and derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: nothing.
