A worker view's writes cannot carry what it read to somebody who may not read it (FR-92). A view runs for every member with that member's sight, and its author is not the member: a chat that read a record somebody else wrote may follow instructions hidden in it. A guest's act was applied as the viewer with any arguments the guest gave, so a view could read what Erin may see and write it into a record Lin reads. Now an act a worker view asks for must be named in its manifest, or it is refused `undeclared`. Where the app's sight is total, an act from the view's code applies with its arguments as given. Sight is total when the policy declares no sights, or every kind is seen whole by everybody (`sightIsTotal`). Otherwise an act from code is refused `press-only`, and an act applies only from a press the host itself saw: a trusted click on an element the view drew with `data-act`, judged and applied in the click's own handler before the view hears of it. Its arguments come only from three places. The first is the record the element is bound to (`data-record`, on it or around it), which must be one the view was shown, or the press is refused `unbound`. The second is the manifest's constants for the act: an act may be listed as `{ act, as, constants, record }`, and `data-act` names it by `as`. The third is the fields in the press's `fieldset` that the host read itself, each one the viewer's.

What counts as the viewer's is decided by the host, and the view cannot fake it. Only an `input` event the browser marks trusted is typing, and a view's code can raise no such event, because what it dispatches lands in its own worker's DOM. A field whose value the view set is filled by the view, and a press that would carry it is refused `untyped`. That covers the field's `value` attribute, a later update, a textarea's text and an option's `selected`. A text field the view wrote words into stays the view's while the viewer types after them, until the viewer empties it. A `change` the browser raises when such a field loses focus is not taken for typing. A view that says back exactly what a field shows, or empties it, takes nothing away. Either way an act is applied as the viewer, `via: "view:<name>"`, within the view's allowance of acts, and is undoable like any other. The view hears what became of its press as `pressed` on the event.

A unit test sets the trap with the offers fixture. Erin may see the internal margin review's cost, and Lin may not. With sights on, the cost written from the view's code, by a press whose argument the view put on the button, through a field the view filled, after the view changed what Erin typed, and through a focus-loss `change`, is refused each time. A press bound to a record the view was not shown and a click no person made are refused too. A press with words Erin typed applies as Erin through the view, and is undone. With sights off, a declared act applies from code, and an undeclared one is still refused. `guest-sandbox` gains `--transport=writes`, where Playwright's real clicks and typing run against five hostile views and an honest one. All twelve claims hold in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox, and Lin never reads the cost in any package's summary. The harness found the two holes the rules about focus loss and typing after the view's words now close. Without them, a view that put the cost after each letter Erin typed got it applied by her press.

Compatibility: the wire — additive: `GuestRefusal` gains `undeclared`, `press-only`, `untyped` and `unbound`, and `dom-event` gains `pressed`. A frame or kit guest is held to nothing new. Ops, stored formats, check codes and tool schemas are unchanged.
