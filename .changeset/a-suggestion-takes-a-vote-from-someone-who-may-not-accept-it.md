A suggestion takes a vote from someone who may not accept it, Roadmap f-suggestion-votes (the framework half; Cloud's signed-out visitors, its tidying notice count and the owner's switch are Cloud's). Someone who could see a suggestion and could not make its change — a reader, a visitor by link, a seat signed out on a public app — pressed Accept in the Suggested tray and met a refusal. Now their Accept and Decline are a vote, for or against: an op of the built-in `vote-suggestion` act (`VOTE_SUGGESTION`, `{ suggestion, vote: "for" | "against" | null }`), which every store answers to and no list offers, as `keep-link` is — `mutation` the call, no primitives, the vote on the op (`Operation.vote`), the records the suggestion names as what its sentence read, so a seat kept from one is kept from the vote as it is from the suggestion. One standing vote per person, the latest, an agent's counted as the person it acts for; voting again changes it and `null` withdraws it (`voteSuggestion`); a vote is its voter's to undo, the system's, or a seat's the policy grants every act. `acceptSuggestion` and `declineSuggestion` ask the store the question Accept would meet (`mayDecide`: every call of the suggestion permitted to the seat) and, where the answer is no, keep the vote instead and answer `voted`; where it is yes they make or decline it as ever, and a store with no policy lets everyone decide as it always did. Every entry of `suggestions()` carries `tally: { for, against, mine? }` — the reader's own standing vote as `mine` — so a list sorts on it, and `votesIn` and `tallyOf` read it off a log. The tray says "Your vote was recorded" to a voter and keeps the row, the pressed button saying which way they voted, Withdraw vote beside it; to whoever may decide it shows "4 for, 1 against" beside Accept, kept current as votes land. An app may say `"tidying": { "autoAcceptAt": 4 }` at the top of its document (`GraviewApp.tidying`, `StoreOptions.tidying`, handed to the store by every host that hands it the policy): at that many votes for, the app's own actor makes the suggestion in the deciding vote's own gesture — one batch, one undo — with its lineage naming the suggestion and the voters (`lineage.voters`, at most 200), having rehearsed that the actor would be let: a record a person said to leave alone leaves the vote standing and the suggestion waiting. `graview check` warns `tidying-votes-unreachable` when the number is set and the app has no policy, since then nobody votes. The agent tools' `accept_suggestion` and `decline_suggestion` answer `voted` from such a seat and `list_suggestions` carries each `tally`; no tool is added and no input schema moves. `a-suggestion-takes-a-vote-from-someone-who-may-not-accept-it.test.ts`, `an-app-says-a-suggestion-is-made-at-so-many-votes.test.ts` and `the-suggested-tray-takes-a-readers-vote-and-shows-the-tally-to-who-may-decide.test.tsx` hold it, and the chrome harness presses Accept as a reader on both faces and reads the tally as the keeper (aSuggestionTakesAVoteFromAReaderAndShowsTheTallyToWhoMayDecide). The graview-tidying skill says it. A hosted page carries 1.5 KB more up front (1_544 bytes minified, 486 gzipped; handed a compiled app, 1.4 KB: 1_470 and 479): the act, the vote's place on an op, the gesture's queue and who may take a vote back — the raises in `scripts/budget-raises/a-suggestion-takes-a-vote-*.json` say each entry's bytes, every one measured against a fresh build of main.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — one new optional op field, `vote`, on the op of one new built-in act, with no primitives and no new primitive; a log holding one folds the same on every build, and a build before this one keeps the field on an op it receives and lists the vote as a change that touched nothing. Stored formats — nothing moves: the op is new and absent in an older store, and a lineage may name `voters`, a key an older build keeps as it keeps any it does not know. The wire — a vote travels as an ordinary `call`; a server before this one refuses the act as unknown, so a host reads `capabilities().version` before it offers a vote to a seat. The declaration — a new optional top-level key, `tidying: { autoAcceptAt }`, refused by nothing that compiled before, and one new warning code, `tidying-votes-unreachable`. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged; `accept_suggestion` and `decline_suggestion` gain `voted` in their answer and `list_suggestions` gains `tally`, both additive.

What Graview Cloud does: on this version its readers' and link visitors' Accept and Decline in the tray become votes with no change of its own, its tidying notice may count a suggestion's `tally`, and its owner's switch is `tidying.autoAcceptAt` in the document rather than a setting of its own; votes from seats it signs out are its half to mint.
