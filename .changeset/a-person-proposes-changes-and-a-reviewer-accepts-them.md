A person proposes changes and a reviewer accepts them, Roadmap f-proposals-for-review. Only the app's own actor could leave a change waiting for somebody: a helper who could see a task and was not trusted to close it met a struck-through act and a sentence, and an agent acting for them could do nothing at all.

A grant may now say what its roles may propose and not run: `{ roles: ["helper"], mutations: [], propose: ["close-task"] }` (acts by name or `"*"`, on the grant's `kinds`; a grant that only proposes says `mutations: []`). Reviewing takes no grant of its own: whoever may run the calls decides. `proposes(policy, principal, act, kind)` and `store.mayPropose(call, principal)` say whether a seat may propose a call it may not run; `proposedMutations` is on `@graview/core/tidying`.

`propose(store, principal, calls, intent)` on `@graview/core/tidying` keeps the calls as the same suggestion op the app's own actor makes, with the person as its author and a lineage naming them (`proposedBy`), its basis their words, else "Proposed by Ada". It travels as a call of the store's own `propose-changes` act (`PROPOSE_CHANGES`, `{ calls, intent? }`), which every store answers to and no list offers, and the store makes its op itself: refused, in a sentence, where the seat may neither run nor propose one of the acts, and each call rehearsed as the store would judge it for the reviewer — the act's own guard, the rules, what the seat may see. The same change already waiting is the one that waits. Whoever may run the calls accepts it (`acceptSuggestion`), made as themselves, the lineage naming the proposal and its proposer (`suggestion`, `proposedBy`); a decline answers it in words ("Declined Ada's proposal: …"); its proposer withdraws it (`withdrawSuggestion`). Accepted, it lands in the working state, and reaches readers on the next publish where the app publishes. `isProposal(suggestion)` tells a person's proposal from the app's own suggestion.

An act's form whose seat may propose it and not run it reads "Propose" and keeps the change as the seat's proposal ("Proposed: it waits in Suggested for somebody who may make it."); a record's page offers such an act beside the rest rather than striking it through. The Suggested tray lists a person's proposals apart and first, under "Proposed", each "Proposed by Ada:", with Withdraw in place of a vote for its proposer. An agent acting for somebody who may propose is offered `propose_changes` (`{ calls: { act, args }[], intent? }`), and `list_suggestions` says `proposedBy`. The studio keeps a grant's `propose`. The permissions and tidying skills say how.

The store's own "Propose changes", the proposal it makes, a grant's `propose` and the form's "Propose" cost a hosted page 2.7 KB more up front (2_813 bytes minified, 834 gzipped), and a page handed a compiled app 2.4 KB (2_492 / 737), under raises naming this changeset; the pages face alone 4_120 / 1_191 more, a visitor's form alone 3_909 / 1_127, a visitor's form handed a compiled app 2_968 / 940, every face 7_378 / 2_234, the embed with the studio handed in 6_430 / 1_993 and Cloud's worker 10_754 bytes, each under a raise of its own; the scene face before it draws measured 1_096_224 B (1071 KB).

Compatibility: ops and primitives — a suggestion's op may now have a person as its author, made by the built-in `propose-changes` act, its lineage naming them as `proposedBy` (1–120 characters, which `lineageOf` holds it to); no primitive is new, and accepting, declining and withdrawing are the ops they were. Stored formats — the one lineage key, and nothing moves: a build before this one reads a proposal as any suggestion and keeps `proposedBy` as a key it does not know. The wire — unchanged in shape: a proposal travels as a `call` of `propose-changes`, which a server that predates it refuses as an act it does not have. The declaration and check finding codes — a grant may say `propose`, `grant-act` reads it too (at `policy.grants.<n>.propose`), `add-grant` and `remove-grant` take it, and a grant may name no act to run where it names acts to propose; nothing that compiled before is refused. Derived tool names and input schemas — `propose_changes` is new, listed only for a seat whose policy lets it propose an act it may not run, so a surface whose policy names no `propose` keeps its tools and its hash; `list_suggestions` carries `proposedBy` on a proposal. What Graview Cloud does: takes this build, lets an owner say who may propose in its permissions editor, and draws its review queue from `suggestions()` with `isProposal`, accepting as the reviewer and declining or withdrawing with the functions above.
