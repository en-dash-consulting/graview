An owner can move a person bound to the wrong record, and what they did, to the right one. An account bound to the wrong person who had acted there stayed so: taking the binding back is refused once its seat has acted as the record, and an ended binding keeps both the account and the record from anybody else. `rebindPerson(store, person, record, { author })` on `@graview/core/binding` runs a new act the framework keeps for itself, `rebind-person` (`{ seat, from, to }`), as the host's system seat, the seat that binds:

- the seat is `record` from now on (`$me`, `own`, `self`), and the record it was bound to is nobody's again, which `mayBind` says may be bound and `bindPerson` binds;
- every act the seat did as the old record is said as the new one from the move on, and counts as the new record's wherever a binding asks who acted as whom; the app as it stood before the move (a past at an earlier seq) still says what was true then;
- what the person did as the old record stays theirs to take back: another member who is that record now may not undo it, and whoever could before still may;
- it is one change, which undo takes back whole: the old binding, and who did what. Taking it back is refused, in words, once the record it left is somebody else's, and once the person has acted as the record they moved to ("Bud has acted here: undoing the move would hand what they did to someone else."), as an unbinding is.

A binding, an ending or a move stands aside for undoing what was done before it: it says only whose record a person is, so an earlier edit of either record is as open to undo as it was before, and nobody is offered the binding as a change to bring along. The log stays append-only: the move is one op of its own, and who did what is read forward through it, so no earlier op is rewritten. A store loaded from a snapshot, or compacted, reads who did what from the bindings its records held where its log begins. What the acts wrote stays as they wrote it. The record moved to must be of the people, nobody's and not set aside (a record set aside is refused by `bindPerson` and `mayBind` too, until it is brought back); anything else is refused in a sentence a host can show. Every other seat is refused: the owner, the person moved, an agent, the app's own actor and a workflow. `graview check`, a compile and an edit refuse a grant that names `rebind-person` (`grant-gives-binding`) and an app's own act of that name (`binding-act-taken`), as they do `bind-person`. `REBIND_PERSON` and `isBindingAct` are on `@graview/core`. The `graview-sight` skill names it. Held by a unit test of core (`an-owner-can-move-a-person-to-the-right-record`).

Compatibility: the declaration and check finding codes — `rebind-person` is a name the framework keeps: an app's act of that name is refused as `binding-act-taken`, and a grant of it as `grant-gives-binding`; no code is added. Ops and primitives — the move is a `rebind-person` op of two `patch-node` primitives on the seat mark; a later binding op no longer stands in the way of undoing an earlier op that wrote no seat mark, so an undo refused before may now pass. Stored formats, the wire, and derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged; the act is offered by no list and no agent tool. What Graview Cloud does: call `rebindPerson` from the host's own seat where an owner says a member was bound to the wrong person.
