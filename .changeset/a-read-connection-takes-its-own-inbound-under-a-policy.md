A read connection takes its own calendar's changes under a declared policy, and a change coming in that the store refuses is said as refused, not as a calendar it could not reach (FR-181). `SyncEngine.guard()` answers the store's `permits` for every surface, and it had no exemption for the mapping's own system: with a policy declared, the engine's own create and `edit-<kind>`, authored by `google-calendar`, met the read-only sentence, and the pass reported "could not reach the calendar; will try again", because the engine read every throw in its inbound pass as the remote being offline. `permits` now lets the mapping's own system through as `admit` already did, while a person, an agent or a view is still refused with the sentence. A change coming in that the store refuses — a policy, an invariant, a host's own `admit` — is caught where it lands and said in `SyncReport.refused`, which now holds `SyncRefusal`s (`{ localId, error, resource?, id? }`, the remote's resource and record id for a change coming in, and `localId` the id a new record would have had), and the pass goes on; only the remote failing is `offline`. `sayReport` names such a refusal by the calendar's event. Choosing a side with `resolve` still throws a refusal to whoever chose. `capabilities().shipped` gains FR-181.

Compatibility: ops, stored formats, the declaration and derived tools are unchanged. The wire is unchanged; `capabilities().shipped` lists FR-181. `SyncReport.refused` entries may carry `resource` and `id`, and `SyncRefusal` is exported from `@graview/core/sync`. What Graview Cloud does: drop its interim stand-in for the connection's system past `permits`, and read a refused inbound change from `report.refused` where `offline` is false.
