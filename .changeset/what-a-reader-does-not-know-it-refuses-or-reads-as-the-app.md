The formats fail closed for 1.0: what a reader does not know it refuses, or reads as the app, and two writers on one log fail loudly. A fold switched on the five primitives with no default, so a build that met a primitive a newer one wrote skipped it and folded a different graph from the same log without a word. Now an unknown primitive is refused with `NewerFormatError` (`unknown: 'primitive "splice-text"'`) wherever ops are folded — opening a log, receiving or rebasing onto ops, `Graph.applyPrimitives` (all or nothing), a seat's view of the log, a migration's fold, `upgradeOp` — and `PRIMITIVE_OPS` names the five. A field value that is an object with a `$type` is a typed value, reserved as `$archived` is: applying one whose type the build does not know (`VALUE_TYPES`, none yet) is refused and the record left as it was, while the log, every adapter, an export and the wire pass it through as written. An author's kind is open the other way: `AUTHOR_KINDS` adds `visitor`, which `formVisitor` now mints for one answer to a form (it said `human`), and a kind a build does not know reads as `system` for display and policy (`authorKindOf`) and is never refused; Activity, history and a link's provenance say a visitor as a person, and a seat header naming an unknown kind is a person's seat. An op's `intent` is display only: ship read a migration's version out of its sentence, and now reads it from its batch. Refusal codes are open: `REFUSAL_REASONS` adds `unauthenticated` (401) and `quota` (403), which a host's `limit` or `admit` says as `{ refuse, reason }` (`HostRefusalReason`; `limit`, 413, when it names none), and a client treats a code it does not know as a final refusal, as `openRemote` already did. `PersistenceAdapter.appendOps(scope, ops, { head })` takes the head the writer last knew, and the memory, SQL, SQLite, file and browser adapters refuse an append that finds the log elsewhere with `LogHeadMovedError`, appending nothing; `openStore` passes it on every write and reports the refusal through `onPersistError`, writing nothing after it, so a second tab or process no longer writes a second op 1 that made the next open refuse the store. A `SyncState` is stamped `format: SYNC_STATE_FORMAT` and a newer one is refused. `scripts/lib/surfaces.mjs` now names sync, subscriptions and their file, a host's views and their file, presence and the refusal codes, and `docs/stability.md` says the JavaScript APIs are held to semver from 1.0. Core's tests refuse a log with `splice-text` instead of diverging, refuse a `$type` it does not know and keep one it only passes along, read a visitor's op as a person's and an unknown kind as the system's, and stamp and refuse a sync state; the adapter contract refuses the second of two writers at the same head on every adapter; ship's tests fail the second of two stores on one scope over memory, a file, localStorage and IndexedDB, and take a refusal code a client does not know as final on the socket and over HTTP. The checks are on every page with the store: about 1.1 KB more up front on a hosted page and one handed a compiled app, 0.8 KB on the pages face alone and 0.4 KB on a visitor's form alone, under raises of those budgets by that (`scripts/budget-raises/formats-fail-closed-*.json`), and the scene face's limit before it draws moves to 979 KB; Cloud's worker imports stay under 2 MB.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — additive: no primitive and no field is added; a primitive or a typed value a build does not know is refused rather than skipped, and every log this build or an earlier one wrote reads and folds as before. Author kinds — additive: `visitor` is new, and a visitor form's ops say it where they said `human`; a reader before this one reads such an op's author as a kind it does not know. Stored formats — additive: `SyncState.format` is new and optional, and a state without it is format 1; `appendOps` takes an optional third argument, and an adapter that ignores it works as before. The wire — additive: `unauthenticated` and `quota` are new codes, said only when a host's `limit` names them; the rule that no code is added within a major is replaced by "a client treats an unknown code as final". What Graview Cloud does: says `{ refuse, reason: "unauthenticated" | "quota" }` from its `limit` where it said `limit` with a sentence, and keeps a default in any `switch` on a refusal's reason or an author's kind.
