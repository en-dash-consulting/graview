A change is said in words. The derived edit's sentence read `Change Billing across apps: stage → "considering"` — the field's key, an arrow and the value as it is kept — and that is what a person reads in Take back and in Activity, and hears when another seat's change is said aloud. It says "Changed the stage of Billing across apps to Considering" now: each field by its label, each value as the record page says it (a choice by its option's words, money and days in the app's currency and locale, a length of time as one, a file by its name, prose by its start in quotes, a record in another app as "a linked record", a date that repeats as "to repeat every week"), and several fields in one sentence — "Changed the price of Model Y to $49,900, the condition to Certified pre-owned and the VIN to “8C9D”". A test holds it over the derived edits of every example app and every document fixture: no sentence has a key, an arrow or a value in a program's quotes.

A hosted page's first load carries 1.0 KB more up front (the sentence is written as the change is applied there), raised in `scripts/budget-raises/a-change-is-said-in-words-a-hosted-page.json` and `…-a-hosted-page-handed-a-compiled-app.json`.

Compatibility: an op's `intent` for a derived edit (`edit-<kind>`) reads differently from this version on: a host that shows it, matches it or stores it beside its own records sees "Changed the … of … to …" where it saw "Change …: … → …". Ops already in a log keep the sentence they were written with. Ops, stored formats, the wire, the declaration and derived tool names and input schemas are unchanged.
