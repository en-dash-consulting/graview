A routed page can be read over the Scene's own city as one face, the page itself moving on one camera between three levels (Graview HQ feature:f-page-zooms-out-to-the-city). A `PagesApp` handed `views` and `sceneUnderPages: true` mounts the Scene under its routes, in the provider the pages already draw with. Page is the page under the bar. Nearby is the same page as a panel beside its place in the city (a sheet from the foot on a phone), with a line from each part that shows a record to that record's building and a Connected list of what it is linked to. City is the whole city, with every page and view of the app standing on its district (the home and views of several kinds on a plaza at its back edge) and the page you came from shrunk into its own. Each step is drawn: the page is moved and scaled, never turned or sheared, and the camera is aimed once before a move and goes straight there.

There is one face, so the bar offers no Scene/Pages switch, no "In the scene" link and no Up. The bar stays fixed at the window's top at every level. One control at the bottom left, Page · Nearby · City and a Key, says where you are and goes to any level. Alt and the arrows move a level, and Escape comes down one (at the City it lets go of the chosen view). Anything chosen at the City (a view, a building, a Find result) lands at Nearby. At the City the camera is free: a pinch or ctrl and the wheel move closer and back without going down. At Nearby the panel's inner edge (a phone's sheet's top) drags wider or narrower, or steps by the arrow keys, and the city follows on the same camera. The width is kept in this browser for the moves between levels. The Scene's record "drive-in" is not drawn on this face, and what it said under a record is the Connected list. While the history is played, a building rises as the scrub passes the change that made it and sinks as it passes the one that removed it, the same element throughout, and the timeline stands beside the control at every level. A line leaves the page from whatever drew the record: a page's row, a lens's event or card, or a view's link.

`graview check` now names two views that show the same thing (`view-duplicates-another`): a view that is only a kind's list, or two views drawing the same picture under two titles. It says which to keep and carries the `remove-lens` edit that removes the other. docs/pages-and-lenses-are-one.md sets out how pages and lenses become one view, the format upgrade that moves a stored document, and what Graview Cloud changes.

Lines keep to the city at every frame: each leaves the page's edge past whatever stands there and runs clear of the control, the legend over it and the timeline beside it, each read as its own rect in the frame that draws it; a line with no way through is not drawn, and a line's end keeps clear of the panel's edge. The City is fitted beside what stands at the foot (above it, or to its right and above the control) and fitted again when the legend is opened or put away there. A view stands on its own district at every width: under its name where that is nearest its own ground, else behind or beside it. On a phone the legend starts put away, and once opened it is kept open. The control's tooltips come on intent: after the pointer rests 600 ms, at once on the next stop for 300 ms after one has shown, at once on the keyboard's focus, and on a long press by a finger. Put away, the control is the same control made shorter: one row at its full height with the level's name (which opens the legend), a track with the thumb inside its ends, and the way back to all three levels; every target is 24 pixels at a desk and 44 on a phone. The page keeps room at its foot only where the control would stand over its words when scrolled to the end, never an empty band. The Scene's menu of a district's views is not drawn on the one face, where each view stands as its own billboard. The bar comes first in the document at every level, so Tab reaches it before the page, and a page that opens at Page leaves the keyboard where the routed face puts it; the city takes no press while it moves between levels. While the one face's own part is fetched the routed page is drawn as it always is, never a blank window, and a change made in that moment is still offered back once the one face draws it again. The legend's keys line says whether the level's single keys are on, with one button that turns them off or back on; off, it says Alt and the arrows still move a level. In the Find box the down arrow goes onto the first thing found, and Enter goes there; a result chosen after typing takes the search's place in the history, so Back goes where Find was opened. At the City that lands at Nearby on the record or place chosen.

Tests: `a-page-stands-on-its-place-in-the-scenes-city` (pages) and `two-views-that-show-the-same-thing-are-found` (core). The `one-face` harness (`pnpm verify one-face`) holds the rest.

Compatibility: the look is unchanged unless `sceneUnderPages` is given; Things and the discography give it, and seedbed and rota with `?city=1`. A person at the root of Things or the discography is sent to the one face. While the history is played, the Scene's buildings rise and sink on every face. At the page a pinch is the browser's own zoom, and Cmd or Ctrl with plus or minus is never taken. On every routed face, the Find box's down arrow goes onto the first result, and a result chosen after typing in the box replaces the search page in the history (Back returns to where Find was opened). The JavaScript API is additive: `PageContext.sceneUnderPages`, `SceneProps.camera` and the `SceneCamera` type, `LayoutOptions.standing` and `plaza`, and `ONE_FACE_LAYERS`. Ops and primitives are unchanged. Stored formats are unchanged. The wire is unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes are additive: `view-duplicates-another`. Derived tool names and input schemas are unchanged.
