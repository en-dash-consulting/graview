The city from altitude is laid out along the ground's own axes, and a relation's ribbon runs on the ground: a straight street beside each row of districts, and at most a bend or two between rows.

The map of #263 stood upstream to downstream left to right across the screen, which on the tilted ground is the lattice's diagonal: every row of districts lay across the grain of the ground, so no street could run along one, and a ribbon between two districts of a row had either to climb a large V past the row or zigzag through the gutters behind each district between. Now `cityMap` lays the chain upstream to downstream along one of the lattice's axes — up and to the right on the screen, as a street in an isometric city runs — each kind one place past the furthest kind it points at, the kinds of one place side by side along the other axis, and the kinds tied to nothing in a row of their own parallel to the rest. Districts stand a block and a cell apart along both axes. In a tall window the chain runs down and to the right, folded into bands, where that stands the city larger. The window is still fitted to the whole city, clear of the chrome and of a panel docked beside the map.

A district's name stands under its own plot, whole or short, and nowhere else: over a plot or beside it, it read as the neighbor's. Where names under their districts would meet, the districts stand further apart along the same axes (`cityMap`'s `pitch`), rather than a name moving. Where no spacing says every name inside the window — a phone, whose names are as wide as its city — the camera starts closer, at the spacing and closeness that name them all with the smallest city, and the city runs past the window's edges, with the signs on those edges saying what is there; a district the person chose is kept in sight. On a phone the panel beside the map is a sheet from the foot, and the city is laid out above it rather than under it (`--graview-sheet`), so what was chosen can still be pressed and asked for its acts; a lens's sheet stands at three fifths of the picture.

A ribbon is routed on the lattice (`routeOnTheGround`): along the ground's axes through the gutters, a cell clear of every curb, never across a district; out of the middle of one district's curb square on and into the other's square on; bending only where it must, so a row's ribbons run its street straight; round the districts' name cards, measured where they were drawn — or, where the cards leave no way at all or only a long way round, under them as a street runs under a sign; keeping to the streets by the districts rather than the open ground beyond them. A stretch another ribbon already runs costs less, so relations that can share a street do, and each keeps one lane along the whole of a street, its own width from the next. The ribbons lie under the districts and their buildings, and follow the tilt the city is seen at, from above as from the side. A district chosen that the app has not got lists nothing beside the map. The router rides in the ribbons' own chunk, fetched when the city is first drawn; the embed's every-face budget is raised by 4_783 / 2_256 bytes for it and the phone's closer camera (scripts/budget-raises/ribbons-run-on-the-ground-every-face.json), and a hosted page's first load is no larger.

Compatibility: every district the declaration does not place by hand stands at a new address once (a declared `plot` stands where it always did), so `graview describe`'s map and a scene thumbnail draw the new layout. `cityMap` takes a `pitch` hint and `@graview/core/city` exports `PITCH`; `placeCity` takes `keep`, the kinds whose districts stay in sight. `routeRibbons`, `tileOf` and `crossesAny` give way to `routeOnTheGround` in `@graview/react`'s ribbon module; none was exported from a package entry.
