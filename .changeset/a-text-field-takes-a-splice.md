A text field takes a splice: the first phase of collaborative text, the primitive and the transform beneath it, with no act, wire or editor yet. A sixth primitive, `{ op: "splice-text", id, field, at, removed, inserted }`, changes part of one text field — at `at`, in UTF-16 code units, the field holds `removed`, and `inserted` takes its place — and `PRIMITIVE_OPS` names it, at op format 3 (`FORMATS.op`; `upgradeOp(op, 2)` reads a format-2 op as written, and a build before this one refuses a store stamped 3, or a splice, with `NewerFormatError`, as #374 made it do). It keeps the text it removed, so its inverse is the same splice turned round (`invert`) and an undo puts the text back; `Graph.applyPrimitives` folds it all or nothing, judges the field's new text as a patch's would be judged, and refuses one whose text is not there — a field that holds no string, `id` or `kind`, an offset past the end, or other text at `at` — as a corrupt op. It writes its record (`writesOf`) and moves its field's revision (`FieldRevisions`), so a whole write made against the text before it is stale, and claims no base of its own. Everything that read a patch reads a splice: the diff of what changed, a seat's view of the log (a splice whose field is kept from the seat is no part of it, one whose text names a record the seat may not see is withheld, and where the seat is served the field otherwise it is said as a patch of the field whole), who wrote each field, a subscription's matched fields (said whole, before and after), an answer's records, a sync engine's first-run values, a migration's fold of a stored graph (`applyToSnapshot`), `graview apply`'s summary, a record's untrusted fields for a model, and a kind's count of changed records. The transform is `@graview/core`'s: `applySplice(text, splice)`, `transformSplices(a, b)` (both outputs, so either order gives one text; where two splices touch, both insertions stay, the one further left first and, at one offset, the one earlier in the log first), `composeSplices(a, b)` (a run of keystrokes as one splice, none when they do not touch), `transformCaret(caret, splice, bias)` and `diffSplice(before, after)` (never halving a surrogate pair). Core's tests hold them by property over seeded cases: 4,000 pairs of concurrent splices converge, 4,000 splices and their inverses put the text back, 300 logs of 25 splices made against older texts fold to one text every time, 4,000 runs compose, 4,000 carets keep their word and 4,000 diffs make one text the other; a format-2 fixture reads as written. The fold and its readers are on every page with the store: 1.9 KB more up front on a hosted page and on one handed a compiled app, and about 1.8 KB on the pages face alone and on a visitor's form alone, under raises of those budgets (`scripts/budget-raises/a-text-field-takes-a-splice-*.json`), and the scene face's limit before it draws moves to 981 KB; Cloud's worker imports measure 2,005,731 B, under a budget raised to 2,010,000.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — additive: `splice-text` is a new primitive at op format 3; every op this build or an earlier one wrote folds as before, and nothing writes a splice yet (the `type-text` act is the next phase). Stored formats — op format 3: a store this build writes is stamped `op: 3`, which a build before it refuses to open with `NewerFormatError` rather than misread; rolling back means refolding from the log on the older build. The wire — a client now says `opFormat: 3`, which every server since 0.1.43 reads as "sends ops bare", and no server sends a splice yet. What Graview Cloud does: takes this build before any of its stores is written by it, since a store stamped 3 does not open on an older worker, and keeps a default in any `switch` on a primitive's `op`.
