The scene keeps up with a hand on a large app, and the ground's grid moves with the picture. A product owner on an app of a thousand records and four thousand links found the scene slow and laggy, and the grid snapping back into place after every pan. A drag sets no React state now: whether a hand or the wheel is moving the picture is a ref the renders read, and what a person sees of the hand — the grabbing cursor, no text selected under it, the signs at the edge put away (`data-graview-hand` on the ground) — is written on the ground outside React, as the transform the hand paints is. The first move of a drag no longer draws the whole scene, and not one commit happens while the hand moves. The lattice the city stands on is an element of its own now, the last on the ground (`.graview-lattice`, where the ground's `::after` was), and a hand dragging the picture says how far it has taken it on that element alone (`--graview-hand-x`/`-y`, added to the lattice's anchor), in the same frame as the transform and taken off in the commit that moves the anchor by as much: the grid moves with the cards on it while they are dragged and does not jump when they are let go, and the frame costs one element's style, not the two thousand a custom property on the ground would reach. The fine square measure under the cards on the ground is part of the picture too: it was the ground's `::before`, fixed to the window while the cards slid over it, and is now an element of its own, the first on the ground (`.graview-measure`), anchored where the world is moved to — a layout says its pan at every altitude now (`Layout.pan`, tweened by `interpolate` with the cards) — and moved by a hand in the same frame as the cards; its fade stays the window's. With the ground's grids no longer pseudo-elements, axe can judge the contrast of what stands in the scene, which it had been reporting as undecidable, and it found the "+N more" card's words 4.0:1 on the receded shelf: they are in ink now, not its muted tone. Letting go of a drag, or the wheel coming to rest, draws the scene once: `useAnimatedLayout` draws a cut in the render that asks for it, rather than putting it into state from an effect a render later, a hand's pan is cut to without the second look a cut otherwise gets (`TransitionOptions.settle`), since the offset was painted where the cut puts every card before the hand let go, and turning motion back on with the layout it has just cut to no longer flies a half-second tween from that layout to itself — thirty-odd renders of the same picture after every drag. The pan no longer lays the city out again (the layout is keyed on the stop less its pan, by what it holds), and the cards in the scene and what floats over the picture — the panel beside the map, the menu at the pointer, the quick ask, the past — read the context free of the pan (`PanFree`, `usePanFreeContext`): kept while only the pan moves, so a board of 250 cards is not drawn again to be shown somewhere else, and a write of the whole view from in there keeps the pan the view has now. A presence's `sharing` is kept while it says the same, so a pan is not a new context for everyone; a band's card and the "+N more" card are drawn again when their picture changes, not when a pan moves them. What a selection reaches is worked out once per selection however many cards ask, its rules judged once per change of the graph — choosing a district had every card walk every edge and judge every rule for itself — and a record's connections read the rules judged once too. A plot drawn again for a zoom names its blocks from the names it said before; the scene stops asking where the ground is on every move of a drag, and says where the hand let go when it does; and the embed's `data-graview-drawn` no longer comes off for a pan, which is not another place (the place watched is the stop less its pan; `onDrawn` is not told the same place again). Measured on a made-up field survey of the same shape at a quarter of the CPU's speed: letting go of a drag 306 ms of main thread before, about 120 after; the wheel 366 → 125; letting go over a board of 250 records 307 → 110, nothing on the board drawn again; a drag's forty moves 413 → 365 with the grid now moving in them; choosing a district lost the 0.66 s every card spent working out what the selection reached. The pages and a hosted page carry 0.3 KB more up front (the presence's kept `sharing`, the drawn signal's place, the layout's `pan`), under raises of the hosted page's budgets, the pages face alone's and a visitor's form alone's (`scripts/budget-raises/the-scene-keeps-up-with-a-hand-*.json`), and the scene face's limit before it draws moves to 999 KB. React's tests hold that a cut is drawn in the render that asks for it and once, that nothing flies to where it already is, that what is inside `PanFree` is not drawn again for a pan and keeps the camera when it writes the whole view, and that what a selection reaches is worked out once for twenty cards. `pnpm verify scene-at-scale` serves the survey with `graview preview` and holds, against a reference measured in the same page: no React commit while a drag moves, from altitude and on the ground; every grid under the picture — the lattice and the measure from altitude, the measure on the ground — moving exactly as far as what stands on it mid-drag, and none of it moving when it is let go; letting go drawing nothing inside a card, under its budget; the wheel likewise; and going into a district of 250 records under its budget.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, the declaration and check finding codes, derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. The JavaScript API — additive: `PanFree` and `usePanFreeContext` on `@graview/react`, and `TransitionOptions.settle`; inside `PanFree`, and inside the scene's cards, `useGraview().view` carries no `pan` (the pan is the scene's). The JavaScript API also gains `Layout.pan` and `InterpolatedLayout.pan` on `@graview/layout`. The theme — the ground's lattice is `.graview-ground > .graview-lattice`, no longer `.graview-ground::after`, and its fine measure `.graview-ground > .graview-measure`, no longer `.graview-ground::before`; a host's own rule over either moves with it. The embed — `data-graview-drawn` and `onDrawn` no longer go round again for a pan alone. What Graview Cloud does: takes this build; a host that styles the ground's grids itself moves its rules to `.graview-lattice` and `.graview-measure`.
