A change is acked only once it is durable, and the host's flush is handed what to write. `liveProtocol`'s `flush` was handed nothing, so a host that writes ops — Graview Cloud commits them to its ledger — found what landed by a high-water mark of its own; and a flush that rejected rejected out of `receive`, so the client heard nothing, its call hung, and the op stayed in the store as if it were durable. Now `flush(landed)` is handed every op the protocol landed that is not durable yet, in seq order — what just landed, after any a failed flush left — and flushes run one at a time. When one rejects, the client is told: refused `unavailable`, "Your change could not be saved just now. It is kept, and sent again until it is.", on the socket and as a 503 over HTTP, and it keeps the change and sends it again. The socket's cursor does not move and `receive` answers no `landed`. The ops stay in the store — taking them back would leave every socket that was already pushed them ahead of the log — but are held back from every socket from then on until a flush holds them: the change sent again is flushed again and acked only once it holds, and the next change hands them over first, with what was held back going down before its ack. So a change sent again after a failed flush is never made twice, and never acked for something that is not durable. `createStoreHandler({ store, flush })` takes the same `flush`, and catches every socket up once held-back ops become durable.

Compatibility: additive for every host whose flush resolves: a `() => Promise<void>` is still a `flush`, and is now handed an argument it may ignore. A host whose flush rejected saw `receive` (and the handler's route) reject or answer 500; it now sees the call refused `unavailable`, a 503 over HTTP, and the client keeps the change — the `unavailable` reason, and what a client before it does with it, are as the change that added it says. `WIRE_PROTOCOL`, ops, stored formats and check codes are unchanged.
