An app says who it is for and what they come to do (Roadmap f-app-jobs). Nothing in a declaration said whom it served or what they come to it to find out, so every look was assembled field by field from a type table and a model was told the kinds and the acts and nothing about the people. A document takes `purpose` now, and `defineApp` the same shape: `for`, a sentence ("The couple and their planner, mostly on phones"), and up to twelve `jobs`, each a question in the people's words (`ask`) answered by the records of one `kind` a rule-language condition holds for (`where`, the record as its subject, read in a TypeScript app exactly as in a document), in an order (`sort`, a field, a computed field or `label`, or `{ by, direction }`), with the act people usually take next on one (`act`), for some `roles` or everybody. `graview check` holds each to the declaration, in a document at `purpose.jobs.<n>.<key>` and in a TypeScript app at `defineApp(…).purpose.jobs[<n>]`: a kind the app does not have (`job-kind`), a `where` that does not read or names what the kind does not have (`job-where`), an order by nothing or by a list or a picture (`job-sort`), an act the app does not have or one that acts on another kind (`job-act`, `job-act-kind`), a role it does not know (`job-role`), a name used twice (`job-name-taken`) and more than twelve (`jobs-too-many`). A page reads `purpose` loosely, as it reads `connections`, so a hosted page carries nothing more up front; its shape is the checker's (`PurposeSpec`) and the document's JSON schema says it to an editor. Four edits change it — `set-purpose`, `add-job`, `edit-job`, `remove-job` — each judged as the checker judges and said ("A new job, "What's cheap?": answered by the vendors where cost < 500."), and the edits that rename or remove carry jobs along: a renamed kind, field, relation or act is renamed in a job's kind, `where`, order and act; a removed kind takes its jobs, a removed field or relation takes the jobs whose `where` reads it and the order by it, and a removed act leaves the job asking without it. `@graview/core/jobs` is a new entry: `jobsOf(app)` (each order with its direction, the kind's plural beside it), `jobsFor(jobs, principal)` (a seat's roles) and `answerJob(store, job, principal)`, which answers `{ count, ids }` read through `store.seenBy(principal)`, so a record kept from the seat is never counted or named and an archived one is set aside. `graview describe` opens with "Who it is for" (the sentence and each job's ask, only a seat's own under `--as`), and `aiContextOf` takes the app's `purpose` and tells a model `for` and the jobs whose roles include the viewer's and whose kind they may see (`state.purpose`); the inline AI hands it the app's (`InlineAsk.purpose`). The studio carries an app's purpose through, a renamed kind's jobs following it; `toDocument` writes it, leaving out a next act the document could not carry. The todo example says it is for a household and asks "What's late?" and "What still needs a slot this week?". The graview-new-app skill says it. Core's tests hold that a purpose is read from a document and from `defineApp` alike and given back, that each code is said at its path, that a job is answered in its order held to the seat's sight, and that every edit carries jobs along or lets go of what a removal takes.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. The declaration — additive: `purpose` on a document and `GraviewApp`, and `set-purpose`, `add-job`, `edit-job` and `remove-job` among the edits. Check finding codes — additive: `job-kind`, `job-where`, `job-sort`, `job-act`, `job-act-kind`, `job-role`, `job-name-taken`, `jobs-too-many`. The JavaScript API — additive: `AppPurpose`, `JobDeclaration` and `JobSort` on `@graview/core`; the `@graview/core/jobs` entry (`jobsOf`, `jobsFor`, `answerJob`, `jobIsFor`, `MOST_JOBS`); an optional `purpose` on `AiContextOptions` and `InlineAsk`, and `purpose` on `AiContextState`; a "Who it is for" section at the head of `describeApp`. What Graview Cloud does: takes this build; `describe_app` says an app's purpose through `describeApp` as it is, a seat's model is told it once Cloud hands `aiContextOf` the app's `purpose`, and `propose_change` takes the four new edits through `editDocument`.
