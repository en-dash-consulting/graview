An app stands on a ground of its own. Apps on Graview Cloud that each chose an accent still looked alike, every one on the same off-white, because the brand could set the accent, the scheme, the faces, the shape, a hue per kind and the logo, but not the page background. A document's brand takes `ground` now: one color, the light scheme's (`"#eef3f8"`), or one for each scheme (`{ "light": "#f4efe6", "dark": "#1a1612" }`), and `set-brand` sets it, `null` going back to Graview's ("The app's page background becomes #eef3f8."). What stands on the ground is drawn from it — the panel a card is, its muted fill, the edge, the bar and a floating sheet — so a card still stands off it; a light ground alone gives the dark scheme its hue taken down to a ground (`darkGroundFor`), never the light color itself. Views read it through the tokens they always did (`--graview-ground`, `--graview-panel`, `--graview-panel-muted`, `--graview-edge`, `--graview-float`). A ground is judged as an accent is (FR-126): one on which ink, muted ink or the accent drops below 4.5:1, on the ground or on a card, in either scheme, is refused by `set-brand` and warned of by `graview check` (`brand-ground`), with the pair, its ratio and the nearest shade of that ground that passes — "Muted ink #586174 on the ground #9a9a94 is 2.1:1; 4.5:1 is needed — #dcdcda would pass." — and a page keeps Graview's ground rather than draw one nobody can read on. The accent is judged on the app's ground: `accentProblem(accent, ground)` and `documentSchemes(accent, ground)`, and a ground that would leave the app's accent unread is refused with the accent's shade that would read. A TypeScript brand builds on one with `brandFromAccent({ accent, base: groundSchemes("#eef3f8") })`, and its contrast is measured as any palette's. A hosted page carries 2.2 KB more up front, and one handed a compiled app 2.0 KB more, under raises of their budgets (`scripts/budget-raises/an-app-stands-on-a-ground-of-its-own-*.json`), and the scene face's limit before it draws moves to 1003 KB. The graview-brand skill says it. Core's tests hold that the ground is kept as given and drawn from, that the dark ground is the light one's hue and not the light one, that ink, muted ink and the accent read on the ground and on a card in both schemes, that `null` gives back Graview's schemes, and that a ground refused names the pair, the ratio and a shade that is then taken; `pnpm verify brand` holds that a ground of the app's own is drawn on both faces in both schemes, the derived one in the dark, with a card apart from it (`theAppsOwnGroundIsDrawnInBothSchemes`).

Compatibility: ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, derived tool names and input schemas, the theme's tokens and the embed — unchanged. The declaration — additive: `brand.ground` on a document and `ground` on `set-brand`. Check finding codes — additive: `brand-ground`. The JavaScript API — additive: `groundSchemes`, `darkGroundFor`, `groundProblem`, `groundReads`, `Ground` and `GroundRefusal` on `@graview/core`, and an optional `ground` on `accentProblem` and `documentSchemes`. What Graview Cloud does: takes this build; its settings picker offers a page background beside the accent and sends `set-brand` `ground`, showing the refusal's sentence and offering its shade.
