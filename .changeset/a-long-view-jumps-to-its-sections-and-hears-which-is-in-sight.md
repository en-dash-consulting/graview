A long view jumps to its own sections, hears which one is in sight, and links to a record's files, Roadmap f-views-scroll-and-anchors. From the Farm Bureau POM Workshop's agenda and TECH N9NE's eras: in a view's worker an anchor link, `scrollIntoView` and the address's hash were refused, a view heard no scroll and had no IntersectionObserver or animation frame, and the kit refused `scroll-margin-top` — so a sticky timeline could neither jump nor light the part a person was reading.

The host does it now. `<a href="#eras">` is drawn — an `href` that names an element of the view's own, and goes to no address — and a press on it scrolls that element to the top of what scrolls, below whatever the page holds stuck there (the one bar, a face's sticky header), keeps the element's own `scroll-margin-top`, gives it the keyboard and leaves the page's address alone. The elements jumps name, and any marked `data-sight`, are watched as the page scrolls: the one in sight is marked `data-in-sight`, each jump to it `aria-current="location"`, and the view hears `sight` on it (`graview.on("sight", "section", …)`). `<a data-file="<record id>" data-field="contract">` is given the address the page serves that record's file at, from a record the view was shown, opening in a tab of its own, or with `data-download` downloading under the file's own name; one that names nothing it was shown is drawn with no address and said. The view kit keeps `scroll-margin-*` and `scroll-padding-*` longhands. All of it is fetched only when a view first draws a jump, a watched section or a file link; a page's first load is unchanged. The graview-view-links and graview-view-drawing skills say how. Tests: `a-view-jumps-to-its-own-sections-and-hears-which-is-in-sight`, and the guest sandbox's presses transport in Chromium and WebKit, on a page with a sticky bar and on the pages face.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged. Stored formats — unchanged. The wire — a view hears a new event, `sight`, on the section in sight; an `<a href="#id">` it draws is kept where it was refused. The declaration and check finding codes — unchanged. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged.

What Graview Cloud does: nothing to change for a view it hosts to jump, light the section in sight and link to a record's files — the host draws it. Its authoring guide and `propose_view`'s hints can stop telling a chat that anchors and `scroll-margin-top` are refused, and point at `href="#id"`, `data-sight` with the `sight` event, and `data-file` / `data-download` instead.
