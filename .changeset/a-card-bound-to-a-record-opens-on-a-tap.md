A card a worker view binds to a record opens on a click, a phone's tap and Enter, not only an `<a>`. Graview Cloud's Threat Watch drew its home as cards — `<div class="card" data-record="threat:…">` with a heading, badges and a line inside — and `describe_place` listed them as record cards, yet the host made a link only of an `<a data-record>`, so a press on a card opened nothing, and on a phone a tap on its heading did nothing at all; its authors worked around it with `data-open` and `graview.on("click", "[data-open]", e => graview.navigate(…))`, `pointer-events: none` on the children and `touch-action: manipulation`. Now any element with `data-record` or `data-place` is a link the host follows, from wherever inside it the press lands: the host walks from the element pressed up to the nearest link, so a tap on a card's heading or a badge opens the card, and a press that lands on something inside with a press of its own — a button, a field, a `data-act`, a lightbox, a drop target, a control's `role`, another link — is that thing's alone, so a "More" toggle the view binds with `graview.on("click")` toggles and opens nothing. An element is no link when it is a press scope: a `fieldset` or `role="group"`, an element with `data-act`, `data-lightbox`, `data-drop` or `data-field`, a field, or one that holds a field to type into or pick from (a checkbox leaves its row a link). The host gives a link the keyboard (`tabindex="0"`) and makes it a link to assistive technology unless it is a list's item, a table's row or says a role of its own, and the region draws every link with `cursor: pointer`, which is what iOS makes a tap a click on, `touch-action: manipulation`, so a tap is not held for a double tap's zoom, and a faint tap highlight. Each element is looked at again after every batch the view draws, since what a card holds is known only once it is drawn whole. How a tap reaches a view, now said in the guide: a phone makes a tap a `click`, the host hears it on the view's shadow root and follows a link first, then tells the view, whose `graview.on("click", selector, fn)` is handed `event.target`, what the finger landed on, and `event.element`, the element the selector matched at or above it.

`guest`'s tests draw Threat Watch's card, a row, a place's section, a fieldset and a card holding a field, and find the card and the row links the host made, followed from the heading, a badge, a line nested deeper and by Enter, the face around the view never hearing the press, a button and a checkbox inside left their own, and the press scopes no links. `node scripts/guest-sandbox.mjs --transport=taps`, a new transport, mounts Threat Watch's home as a host does: in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox the card is a link drawn with a pointer and no double-tap zoom, and a click on its heading and Enter on it open the threat's page; and on a phone (Chromium's and WebKit's mobile emulation; Firefox has none) a tap on its heading and on a badge inside it open the threat's page, and a tap on the view's own toggle toggles it and opens nothing. Before the change a click, Enter and a tap each left the reader on the home. Links in a worker view are a skill of their own, `graview-view-links` — a link, a card that opens, how a tap reaches the view, narrowed and to a section — and `graview-worker-view`, which was near its limit, points at it. The guest host drawing a worker view on the open kit carries 1.7 KB more (1_736 bytes, 738 gzipped), under a raise of that budget (`scripts/budget-raises/a-card-bound-to-a-record-opens-on-a-tap-open-kit.json`); nothing a hosted page loads up front grew.

Compatibility: additive for a view — an element other than an `<a>` with `data-record` or `data-place`, which opened nothing, now opens its record or place on a press and is given `tabindex="0"` and, where it has no meaning of its own, `role="link"`; a view that bound such an element to do something else on a click binds it by `data-key` instead. The wire, the stored formats, the declaration and the derived tools are unchanged.
