A district's "⚠ 3" on the map is a control. It was dead text: the name card said a district had problems and nothing on the map said which. It is a button now, a fingertip tall, named for what it opens ("3 problems in Tasks"), and its hover says the first problem in the rule's own sentence. Pressed, or Enter or Space on it, it chooses the district and brings its problems up at the head of the panel beside the map (the sheet on a phone): the bar's own rows, each in the words the problems list uses, with the number of ways to fix it, and a press that chooses the record it names and opens its acts — the repairs lead. The count is the bar's source, the broken rules that name one of the district's records, so the card, the panel and the bar never say two things; the records they name are outlined on the map while the panel is open, and the keyboard goes back to the count when the panel is put away, never to the page. A district's count says what it counts ("36 rooms") on hover, and a row's "+n" says what it is.

Compatibility: additive. A district card's problem count is a button where it was a span; nothing a host wrote reaches it.
