The shade a ground refusal names is kept when it is sent back (Roadmap f-ground-suggestion-passes). On 0.1.54, `set-brand { ground: { light: "#f4efe6", dark: "#9a9a94" } }` was refused with "#343431 would pass", and #343431 was then refused too, for faint ink on the cards at 3.6–4.2:1: the shade had been judged against ink, muted ink and the accent alone, while the compiled palette (`checkBrandContrast`) holds every pair. A ground is now judged on every pair the check holds a palette to — ink, muted and faint ink, the accent, the status colors and the lit edge, on the ground, the cards, the receded and flagged cards, the floating sheet and the bar, in both schemes — and the shade a refusal names is searched on that judgment with the app's accent kept as set-brand keeps it, so sending it back is kept and its palette passes the check. The refusal names the pair that fails ("In the dark scheme, faint ink #97a0b6 on a card (#…) on the ground #343431 is 3.6:1; 4.5:1 is needed — #… would pass."). A ground's lit edge is drawn at the alpha that clears 3:1 on its cards, as an accent's always was, so an edge is never the reason a ground is refused. A core test sends back every shade named over a spread of light and dark grounds, with and without an accent, and holds each kept with a palette `checkBrandContrast` passes. A hosted page carries 0.8 KB more up front (824 bytes minified, 236 gzipped; 244 handed a compiled app), with f-dark-brand-accent's dark page beside it: the compile draws the brand on every page.

Compatibility: the declaration and check finding codes — `set-brand` and `brand-ground` refuse a ground on which faint ink, a status color or the lit edge does not read, where they judged ink, muted ink and the accent alone; a ground that passed before and whose compiled palette `checkBrandContrast` passed is still kept. Ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged.
