A worker view's `<button>` and its links are plain by default, so the semantic element is the easy one. A view that drew a real `<button>` had the browser's grey box, border and font to undo, so views drew a `<div>` with a click handler instead — reached by no keyboard and nothing to a screen reader. The region now resets every `<button>` to the words around it (`font` and `color` inherited, no background, border, padding or margin, `appearance: none`, a pointer) and a link the host made to its words' color with no underline, both under `:where`, at no specificity, so a view's own class or element rule always wins. A `<button data-record>` goes where it says as an `<a data-record>` does, and on an element bound to an act `data-record` is always that act's record and never a link: `<button data-act="acknowledge" data-record="threat:7">` acknowledges the threat and goes nowhere, which the tests now hold. The guides say it plainly — go somewhere with `<a data-record>` or `<a data-place>`, do something with a `<button>` or `<button data-act>`, style either freely, and draw no `<div>` or `<span>` with only a click handler; a list of records is a list of `<a data-record>`, never `graview.on("click", "[data-id]", … navigate)` — in `graview-worker-view`, `graview-view-links` and `graview-view-forms`, and in `@graview/guest`'s README, whose example heard a button's click to navigate. No view audit exists yet to flag a clickable `<div>`: that is the coming view accessibility audit's.

`guest`'s tests read the region's two zero-specificity rules, follow a `<button data-record>` keeping its own role and tab stop, and press a button bound to an act with a `data-record` of its own and one inside a card bound to another record, each carrying its record and going nowhere. In the taps transport, in three engines, an `<a data-record>` is drawn in the words' own color and not underlined.

Compatibility: a view's `<button>` drawn with no style of its own now looks like the words around it rather than the browser's button; a view that wants the browser's look sets it on its own class. The wire, the stored formats, the declaration and the derived tools are unchanged.
