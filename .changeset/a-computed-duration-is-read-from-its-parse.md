A computed field is said as a length of time because its parsed expression works one out, not because its words looked like it. The face read the expression's text — split at its `+` and `-`, matched against patterns — so `budget - sum(in('sessions'), length)` was said "7 h 45 min" when the sessions point the other way, and a guess about a TypeScript declaration's words decided what a reader was shown. A document's compile now reads each kind's computed fields from their parse, where the parser already is (`durationsOf`, `document/durations.ts`): a duration field of its kind, another computed field that is one, a field across a relation or a sum, least or most of one over `out(...)` or `in(...)` whose far kinds hold a duration in it, two of these added or taken apart, or one negated. The compiled app names them (`KindPlan.durations`), the built definition says `as: "duration"` on each, and a face reads `as` and nothing of the expression — no parser on a page, and the kinds-beside-it index the guess needed is gone from `@graview/core`. A TypeScript declaration has no compile step to read, so it says `as: "duration"` itself; one that does not is said as a number. A definition written back as a document (`toDocument`) leaves `as` out.

Compatibility: `graview-compiled@1` gains an optional `durations` (computed field names) on a kind's plan; a plan without it builds as before and says those fields as numbers, and a build that predates it ignores it. A TypeScript declaration's computed sum of durations without `as: "duration"` is now said as a number. The document format, check finding codes, ops, stored formats, the wire and tool schemas are unchanged.
