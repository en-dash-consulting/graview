The Scene holds steady under a finger and a mouse (Roadmap feature:f-scene-interaction-pass, from the Tech N9ne discography on Cloud: "Scene interactivity is buggy and awkward on mobile (and desktop)"). Every gesture was driven on the discography example — the same catalog, 1,177 songs over five kinds — on a Pixel 7 in Chromium and an iPhone 13 in WebKit, and at a desk with a mouse and a wheel, and seven things were found.

A pinch on an Android phone did not zoom. Chrome hands a pinch over as two touches and nothing else — no ctrl+wheel, no GestureEvent — and with `touch-action: none` on the ground the first finger panned the city while the second re-based the pan under it. The scene reads the two pointers now: the city zooms by how far apart the fingers went, about where they were, and moves as far as their middle moved; on iOS, where Safari also says a pinch as a GestureEvent, the pointers are the one reading while two fingers are down, so the city is not zoomed twice.

A zoom was a new layout every frame — about thirty milliseconds of work per frame on a thousand records at a phone's speed, for a pinch and for ctrl+wheel alike. A hand's zoom is now painted on the world's layers as a transform, as a hand's pan already was, the lattice grown with it, and the city is drawn at its new size once, when the fingers lift or the wheel rests. The zoom's own buttons still step and tween.

On a phone, a tap on a district opens its sheet and the city is fitted again above it, so it moves under the finger: the second tap of a double-tap landed on the zoom's "+", and a quick tap on the next district chose whatever had slid under it. A press within a beat of a tap is now judged against the picture as the tap left it: a press aimed at a card that has moved is that card's, and a finger's second tap on the card the first one chose is the double-tap that goes in, whether or not the browser says a double-click (Safari on a phone does not).

Scrolling a focused district's list on a phone moved the district: its drag began under the finger before the browser took the touch to scroll, and the cancel was read as a lift, so the card was pinned where the scroll left it. A press inside something of a card's that scrolls is the list's on a touch screen, and a `pointercancel` now puts back what the hand moved and writes nothing.

The page behind the scene could be pulled when a list in a card scrolled past its end; the ground now contains overscroll. And with a sheet open on a phone, the window growing by a toolbar folding away read as the sheet rising, and the city was fitted again; the sheet is now read against the box the scene kept. And on a phone the sign for a district past the right edge ("Eras →") stood on a chosen district's showings ("Tracklists"), taking the press meant for it: the layout knew only the showings of a district a lens is open on, so the signs now also step past the showings as they are drawn.

What already held, and is now held by the harness: a drag from bare ground or from a district pans and never chooses, a tap on bare ground never pans, ctrl+wheel zooms under the cursor, a tap draws no focus ring, Up from a focus brings the city back as it stood, and turning a phone keeps the choice.

react's tests hold the pinch (`two-fingers-zoom-the-city-under-them`), the aimed press (`a-press-lands-on-what-was-aimed-at`) and the list's scroll (`a-finger-in-a-list-that-scrolls-scrolls-it`). A new harness, `pnpm verify scene-hands`, drives the discography on a Pixel 7 and an iPhone 13 and at 1440×900 in Chromium and WebKit and writes `docs/scene-hands.json`: `aTapChoosesWithoutARing`, `aTapOnTheGroundNeverPans`, `aDragOnTheSceneNeverSelects`, `aPinchZoomsUnderTheFingers`, `aWheelZoomStaysUnderTheCursor`, `aDoubleTapGoesIntoTheDistrictPressed`, `twoQuickTapsLandOnTheSecondCard`, `aFocusedPreviewScrollsWithoutMovingTheScene`, `upFromAFocusBringsTheCityBackAsItStood`, `turningThePhoneAndItsToolbarMoveNothing`, `thePageNeverScrollsBehindTheScene` and, at a quarter of the processor in Chromium, `panAndPinchKeepTheirFramesAtAQuarterSpeed`. A hosted page's Scene carries 6.3 KB more before it draws (6,486 bytes minified; nothing more up front), and every face and the embed with the studio handed in 2.0 KB more gzipped, raised in `scripts/budget-raises/the-scene-holds-steady-under-a-finger-and-a-mouse-*.json`.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, stored formats, the wire, the declaration and check finding codes, and derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: nothing; every hosted app's Scene, the Tech N9ne discography among them, gets the gestures as they are here on the next build.
