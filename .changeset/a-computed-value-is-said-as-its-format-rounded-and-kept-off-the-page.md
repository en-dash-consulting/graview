A computed value is said as its format, can be rounded, and can be kept off the record's page while it still ranks a list (FR-217), as Roadmap f-computed-format asked. A season's `pct = wins / (wins + losses + ties)` read "0.8235294117647058" under Details: `set-format` refused it ("season has no field pct"), the rule language had no `round()`, and a share worked out only to rank seasons could not be kept off the page a lens sorted by it. Now a kind's computed field takes `format` (`"money"`, `"percent"` or `"duration"`) and a `unit`, as a number field does — `{ "expr": "wins / (wins + losses + ties)", "format": "percent" }` — and the record's page, a card, a row of a list's CSV and a view spec's field block say it so ("82%", "$785,714.29", in the brand's currency and locale); a declaration in code says the same on `defineNode({ computed })`. `set-format` on a computed field sets or clears them (`season's pct is worked out as before and said as a percent, kept as a fraction — 0.15 reads 15%.`), refuses `"markdown"`, and keeps the field short as its bare expression once nothing else is left. `round(x)` and `round(x, places)` round half away from zero, nothing staying nothing, and the checker, the rule's line in words ("… rounded to 3 places") and the rule language's own function list know it. A computed field with `hidden: true` — `set-computed { "hidden": true }`, `false` or `null` to show it again — is left out of what a record says of itself (its page, its card, its CSV columns) and is still worked out for a list's or a lens's sort, a rule, a template and a view. Core's test formats a computed share as a percent and a ratio as money on the page, in the CSV and in a field block, rounds one, and hides one from the page and the CSV while it still sorts the list. The graview-view-specs and graview-invariant skills say it.

Compatibility: ops and primitives, stored formats, derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes: additive — a computed field's long form takes `format`, `unit` and `hidden`, `set-computed` takes `hidden`, `set-format` takes a computed field's name, and the rule language knows `round`; a build that predates it refuses the new keys and reports `round` as an unknown function. The wire: unchanged; `capabilities().shipped` gains FR-217. What Graview Cloud does: lets propose_change's `set-format` name a computed field and `set-computed` carry `hidden`, and mentions `round(x, places)` and a computed field's `format` where it teaches the rule language to a model.
