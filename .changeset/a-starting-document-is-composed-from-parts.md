A starting document is composed from parts (Roadmap f-compose-from-parts, the starter's second framework half). A person who says "kinda" to a template keeps some of its parts, drops others and asks for parts it lacks, and one nothing fits composes from what they described alone; Cloud's starter could only remove kinds. `startingDocument({ base?, drop?, add?, name, purpose? })` on `@graview/core/compose` starts from a template's document or from nothing (one `item` kind, or the `person` kind itself when a roster is asked for), takes each dropped kind out with the rules, lenses, acts and relations only it served (`remove-kind`), takes each dropped trait's own carriers out — its rules, lenses and sights, its relations and fields no other trait names with the lenses drawn by them, and a kind of its own left standing apart — and brings each added trait's module in, joined to the kind people see most rather than standing apart: `owners` a `person` kind (or the people the base has), an `owner` link, "Give it to someone" and the rule that every one has somebody on it, with a coverage of who has what; `status` the field (or the one the base has) with its named steps and a board; `schedule` a due date, "Set the date", the on-time rule and a calendar; `budget` a cost beside the money the base keeps, a budget on the kind the main kind is grouped under (else on itself), "Set the budget", the over-budget rule and the app's currency where it had none; `places` a place kind and where each is; `sources` a source kind, `cites`, "Cite a source" and the rule that each (done one) cites a source; `stock` on hand and a reorder point with the rule that holds them; `votes` a count and "Vote for it"; `private` a sight that keeps the main kind's records to their maker with the rest shown to everyone; `history` a season kind, a date, a result and the grouping; `publishing` the words, a published value and "Publish", "Take it down", "Write it" and the rule that a published one has words; `roster` the people with a role, an email and a phone; `routines` how often, last done, "Done today" and the overdue rule; `comparison` a chosen value, "Choose it" and one chosen per group (or per app). A trait the document already carries is left as it is and said so. What the parts made together is judged by the critic once (a kind now the one people see most with no glance, a thirteenth place) and its own edits applied, so the document is handed on with no warning; `composeDesign` then gives it its jobs and look. The result says what it took from where — `took`, each part as `{ part, what, from }` (a kind, field, relation, rule, lens, act, sight or trait; from the base's name or the trait that brought it) — and `left` what was dropped, for a host to show as chips. Over Cloud's catalog, every module composes onto every template and onto nothing, and dropping any kind or any trait of any template leaves a document that compiles with no warning. Along the way `remove-kind` learned what it left behind for the checker to refuse: it now takes the kind's hue out of `brand.accents` (and `rename-kind` moves it), the sights `via` a relation that went with the kind, and, when the kind was the people, the acts on the acting person's own record (`$me`). On no page: the compose entry is a host's, and nothing a page loads grows but the editor — Cloud's worker 1_232 bytes and every face 73 gzipped bytes more, each under a raise of its own; a hosted page's first load is unchanged.

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged. Stored formats — unchanged. The wire — unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes — no code changes; `remove-kind` takes more with the kind (its hue, the sights through its relations, the `$me` acts of the people kind) where it left each for `brand-accent-unknown-kind`, `sight-via-unknown-edge` and `people-unsaid` to refuse, so a change that removed a kind and then met one of those now goes through, and `rename-kind` moves the hue; what each trait's module is made of is a judgment a version may change. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged.
