An app's own images are drawn under its base, its head offers home-screen icons, and a dark app opens through a dark screen, Roadmap f-brand-marks-under-app-base (Graview Cloud's brief b-cloud-brand-marks-base-2026-10-29), f-app-icons-home-screen and f-splash-follows-declared-scheme, all three from TECH N9NE on Graview Cloud.

A document names its logo, its page icon and its opening screen's image at `/graview/assets/<sha>.<ext>` on the host's root, and every face drew them there, so on a host that serves each app under a base (`https://<workspace>.graview.app/a/<app>/`) a visitor's logo and icon found nothing. `mount`, `<Embed>`, `GraviewProvider`, the Shell and the routed face (`PagesApp`'s context) now take `assetsAt?: (path) => string`, as `filesAt` already says where a record's files are served: every path under `/graview/assets/` the page draws is asked of it — the app bar's and the masthead's mark (each scheme's face), the page's icon, the logo a worker view is handed, and an image a worker view draws by its path where the view's definition says no `asset` of its own. Inline SVG, an emoji, a page-relative path and any other address are drawn as given, and unsaid nothing changes. `assetSrc(assetsAt, href)` and `HOST_ASSETS` on `@graview/core` say it once; `useHostAssets` and `HostAssetsContext` on `@graview/react/provider` carry it to a design's own shell. `splashHtml` takes `assetsAt` too, for the screen's image and the plain screen's mark. The static page renderer already drew a host-root mark under its `base`, and still does. Core's `a-mark-under-an-app-base-is-drawn-there.test.ts` and primitives' `a-logo-under-an-app-base-is-drawn-there.test.tsx` hold it, and verify-brand opens the app at `/a/en-dash/` with its own images kept only there, as a fresh visitor, on both faces in three engines (aLogoUnderAnAppBaseLoadsForAVisitor).

The page's head had an icon link and nothing a phone keeps an app by. A new entry, `@graview/core/head` (on no page up front but `themeColorsOf`), says it from the brand and the base: `homeScreenOf(brand, { base, assetsAt?, name?, png? })` answers the head's tags as HTML — the icon typed by what it is (a PNG is `image/png`, never served as an SVG), an `apple-touch-icon` at 180, `<link rel="manifest">`, and a `theme-color` per scheme with its `media`, one with none where the brand pins a scheme — the web manifest (`name`, `short_name`, 192, 512 and maskable 512 icons, `theme_color` and `background_color` from the brand's ground, `display: "standalone"`, `start_url` and `scope` at the base) and the files a host answers under the base, each saying what it is made from. A PNG mark is every size as it is, or resized by the host; an SVG or emoji mark is no PNG without a rasterizer, so unless the host says `png: true` (it makes them) it is offered as SVG alone — `/icon.svg` and the manifest's `sizes: "any"` — and no `apple-touch-icon` is written. `graview serve` and `graview preview` answer the manifest and `/icon.svg` and send a PNG size to the brand's own PNG (`homeScreenAnswer` on `@graview/ship` and `/runtime`), and `graview preview` writes the tags into its page. A face that owns the page — the Shell, the routed face on its own, an embed told `favicon: true` — puts the app's `theme-color` in the head while it is drawn (`useThemeColor`, on `@graview/primitives`), the page's own put back after. Core's `the-head-offers-home-screen-icons.test.ts` and ship's `a-served-app-offers-its-home-screen-icons.test.ts` hold it, and verify-preview opens a preview whose head offers the icon, the manifest and its every icon each answered as what it says, and the app's one color (theHeadOffersHomeScreenIcons).

TECH N9NE's brand says `scheme: "dark"`, and on a light device its opening screen drew a cream veil with dark ink before the app opened dark. `splashHtml` now opens in a scheme the reader chose where the host says `light` or `dark`; else the one the app declares — the brand's `scheme`, else the screen's own — and a host's `scheme: "auto"` no longer overrides it; else the device's (`splashScheme` says which). `brand.splash.scheme` — `"light"`, `"dark"` or `"device"` — is the screen's own where the brand pins none; `set-brand` takes it and says it ("The app opens on a dark editorial screen while it loads."), and `graview check` says one the brand's scheme overrides (`brand-splash-scheme`, a warning). Core's `an-opening-screen-says-its-scheme.test.ts` and pages' `a-dark-app-opens-through-a-dark-screen.test.ts` hold it, and verify-preview opens a dark app on a device that prefers light: the screen's ground is dark and its words light before the app's code ran, and the app opens dark (aDarkAppOpensThroughADarkSplashOnALightDevice). The graview-logo skill says the base and the home screen, graview-brand the screen's scheme and graview-host-page `assetsAt` in a line.

A hosted page carries 1.4 KB more up front (1_398 bytes minified, 477 gzipped) and a page handed a compiled app 1.3 KB more (1_358 / 471): `assetsAt` through the provider and the frame, `assetSrc`, and the theme-color a face puts in its head, under raises naming this changeset (`scripts/budget-raises/brand-marks-under-an-app-base-and-home-screen-icons-*.json`); the pages face alone 1_434 / 528 more, every face 834 gzipped, the embed with the studio handed in 726 gzipped, the guest host registering a worker view 361 / 166, and Cloud's worker 250 bytes, each under a raise of its own; the scene face before it draws measured 1_097_638 B (1072 KB).

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged. Stored formats — unchanged. The wire — unchanged; `graview serve` and `graview preview` answer `/manifest.webmanifest` and `/icon.svg`, and a PNG size with a 307 to the brand's PNG, where they answered nothing. The declaration and check finding codes — `brand.splash` gains the optional key `scheme`, which a build before this one refuses by path, and one new warning, `brand-splash-scheme`; no declaration that passed is refused. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged; `set-brand`'s `splash` takes `scheme`. `mount`, `<Embed>`, `GraviewProvider`, the Shell, the routed face's context and `splashHtml` gain the optional `assetsAt`; `splashHtml`'s `scheme: "auto"` stops overriding a scheme the app declares.

What Graview Cloud does: for the marks, passes `assetsAt` — the path under `/a/<slug>` — to `mount`, so the app bar's logo and the page's icon load under the app's base, and to `splashHtml`, in place of its own `underBase` rewriting of the splash's image and logo. For the home screen, it writes `homeScreenOf(brand, { base: "/a/<slug>", png: true })`'s `html` into the app page's head and answers its `files` from its worker — the manifest's JSON, `/icon.svg`, and each PNG resized from the brand's PNG or rasterized from its SVG or emoji (`from`), in place of `favicon.svg` serving PNG bytes; serving those files is Cloud's half. For the opening screen, `splashHtml` already draws TECH N9NE's editorial screen dark; Cloud's own `.gc-splash` screen (its owner's shader and its frame) follows `prefers-color-scheme` alone, and should open in `splashScheme(brand)` instead — a `data-scheme` and `u_light` from it — and its opening-screen form may offer `scheme` where the brand pins none.
