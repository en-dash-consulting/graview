A view's thumb can be cropped to a shape around the picture's focus. A grid of square tiles asked for `pictures.thumb: 240` and was handed each picture made smaller and nothing more, so a tile cropped it at its middle and a subject high in a tall picture was cut off. A manifest (or a frame's host) may now say `thumb: { width, aspect }`: each picture's `thumb` is cropped to that width over height around its `focus` (its middle where the record says none), as much of the picture as the shape holds, then made `width` pixels across. A cutout, as the record says it or as the host reads it off the pixels, is never cropped: its thumb is the whole picture at that width. A crop is handed beside `src` even where the picture is no wider than the thumb, since it is a picture of its own. A headless run reads no pixels, so it knows a cutout only where the record says one, and reckons the rest as cropped. `thumb: { width }` alone is `thumb: <width>`, and a number still asks for a thumb made smaller and not cropped. A shape out of bounds (an aspect outside 0.1 to 10, a width not narrower than the view's, another key) is refused by the manifest's check in its own words, `view-pictures-thumb`, and a frame's host says it once as `picture-thumb-unsound`. The `graview-view-drawing` skill says how to ask for one. Held by a unit test of the guest (`a-views-thumb-is-cropped-around-the-pictures-focus`).

Compatibility: the wire — additive: a manifest's `pictures.thumb` may be `{ width, aspect }`, and a view that asks for a number is handed what it was before. Ops and primitives, stored formats, the declaration and check finding codes, and derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. The JavaScript API is additive: `ThumbShape` on `@graview/guest/host/views`, the shape `GuestPictures.thumb` may take. What Graview Cloud does: nothing to keep drawing; a view of tiles may ask for `thumb: { width, aspect }`, and a picture's `focus` set at upload or by a person is where it is cropped around.
