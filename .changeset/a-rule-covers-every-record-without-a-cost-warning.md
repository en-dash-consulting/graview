A rule can cover every record without a cost warning, Roadmap f-rule-covers-every. "Every candidate has a stance on every issue the guide covers" could be written only by counting the distinct issues a candidate's stances reach against `all('issue')`, and the checker rightly said `rule-cost` of it — the evaluator would walk every issue for every candidate — which a curated template may not carry, so the voter guide said only "has a stance on file". A rule now declares the shape instead: `{ "over": "candidate", "covers": { "every": "issue", "through": "stances", "via": "issue" }, "says": "{candidate} has no stance on {issue}" }` — `through` a relation of the rule's kind (or one that points at it) and `via` one more where the first lands on a kind in between, or `through` alone when it reaches the covered kind directly. The store judges it in one pass over the record's links (`uncovered`, a set per record held against the kind's live records), never through the expression evaluator, so there is no budget to run out of and no cost to warn of; each far record the subject does not reach is one problem naming both, and the sentence may name either by its kind beside the subject's fields. Its line reads "reaches every issue through stances, then issue", and broken, "does not reach Parks through stances, then issue". `graview check` says a relation that does not exist or does not lead from the kind to the covered one as `rule-covers-relation`, at `covers.through` or `covers.via` and in the person's voice with the way out ("add "via": the relation from stance to issue"), and a kind that is none as `rule-kind`; a rule that says both or neither of `require` and `covers`, or `covers` over the whole graph, is refused by path. A renamed kind or relation is followed in `covers`, and the rule goes with the kind it covers or a relation it walks. The same rule in TypeScript is `coverageRule` on `@graview/core/document`, which `toDocument` writes as `covers`; the studio keeps one through its round trip (`coversEvery`, `coversThrough`, `coversVia` on a rule) and writes it back as `coverageRule`, and `graview serve`'s source editor adds one beside the checkout's rules. The graview-invariant skill says the shape.

Reading the shape, resolving the walk and judging it costs a hosted page 3.1 KB more up front (3_192 bytes minified, 1_029 gzipped), and a page handed a compiled app 2.2 KB (2_212, 717), under raises naming this changeset; a visitor's form handed a compiled app 2_207 / 724 more, every face 7_700 / 2_258, the embed with the studio handed in 7_154 / 2_117 and Cloud's worker 10_564 bytes, each under a raise of its own; the scene face before it draws measured 1_064_463 B (1040 KB).

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged. Stored formats — unchanged; a compiled app's `RulePlan` may carry `covers` (each step with its direction) and no `require`, which a page handed one from this build reads. The wire — unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes — a rule gains the key `covers` and `require` becomes optional beside it (one of the two, refined by the schema and said again in `document.schema.json`); the finding code `rule-covers-relation` is new and said only of `covers`; an `InvariantDefinition`'s `judgment` and `shape` may carry `covers` and no `require`, so a host that read `judgment.require` as a string reads it as optional; no declaration that passed is refused. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: write the voter guide's rule as `covers` and drop the "has a stance on file" stand-in; a build that predates `covers` refuses it at `rules.<rule>.covers`, so Cloud stores one only on a build whose document schema has it.
