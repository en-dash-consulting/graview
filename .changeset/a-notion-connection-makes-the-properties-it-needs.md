A Notion connection makes the properties it needs, and one database holds several kinds, Roadmap request:syncing-with-notion-needs-to-allow-for-graview-to-autocreate-the-necessary-properties (Nick, from the site). A Notion connection kept one data source as one kind, and a property it mapped that the data source lacked was only said ("no property in the database for Status, so it is not kept in step") — a person set up every column by hand before anything synced, and a database whose data sources were a project's tasks and its rooms took one connection per source, each with its own credentials and passes. Now a connection may name `sources: [{ "source", "kind", "properties"? }]` in place of its own `source`, `kind` and `properties`: one engine of several resources (`pages:<kind>`), each data source read with a cursor of its own and each page written to the source its kind is kept in; a relation between two of them is a Notion relation property, kept as an edge as one between two connections is. And on its first pass, and whenever one goes missing, a connection that writes gives a data source each property a mapped field lacks (PATCH `/data_sources/{id}`), of the type the field is held in — words a text, a choice a select with its options as people read them ("In progress"), several choices a multi-select, a date, a checkbox, a number, a URL, an email, a phone, and a relation one to the source keeping the kind it reaches — named as the declaration names it, or, matched by name, as the field reads ("Due date"); the name field is the title Notion always has. It never renames or deletes a property somebody made, says one of another type and leaves it, makes no status and no people property, and says what it made in the report and the pass's line: "Made 3 properties in Notion: Status, Due and Done" (`SyncReport.said`, new — what the transport did there or found, as sentences, the first time). `create: false` turns it off; a connection that only reads makes none. A record's page says the source that keeps it, "Kept in step with Notion · Tasks", with Open in Notion to its own page where it came from there and to the database otherwise (`keptInStepWith` on `@graview/core/words`; a calendar's says Google Calendar). `graview check` refuses a source of a kind the document lacks (`connection-kind` at `connections.<name>.sources.<n>.kind`), a property mapped to a field the kind lacks (`connection-field` at the source's `properties.<field>`), and, as the new error `connection-sources`, two sources for one kind, one data source named twice, or `sources` beside the connection's own `kind`, `source` or `properties`; a connection with neither is `connection-kind`. `scriptedNotion()` changes a data source's properties as Notion does, and says a source's properties (`properties(id)`). Core's `notion-makes-the-properties-it-needs-and-keeps-several-sources.test.ts`, pages' `a-record-says-the-notion-source-it-is-kept-in-step-with.test.tsx` and ship's `declared-connections-run-beside-the-store.test.ts` hold it against the scripted Notion; the graview-sync skill says the two forms and what is made. A hosted page carries 0.2 KB more up front (200 bytes minified, 66 gzipped) — the compile reading each source's fields — under a raise naming this changeset; a page handed a compiled app carries nothing more; the pages face alone 1_179 / 464 more (the record page's line), every face 3_295 / 1_192 and the embed with the studio handed in 3_295 / 1_189, and Cloud's worker 11_148 bytes, each under a raise of its own; the scene face before it draws measured 1_078_654 B (1054 KB).

Compatibility: ops and primitives — unchanged. Stored formats — a connection of one source keeps its links under `pages` and its cursor as before; one of `sources` keeps each source's links under `pages:<kind>` and its cursor as `{ "by": { "<resource>": … } }`, which a host keeps without reading. The wire — unchanged. The declaration and check finding codes — a Notion connection gains `sources` and `create`, its `kind` is optional when `sources` names each kind, and the checker gains the error `connection-sources` and says `connection-kind` and `connection-field` under `sources.<n>`; a declaration that passed before still passes, and a connection that writes now adds to its data source the properties it mapped and did not find — `create: false` keeps a data source exactly as it is. Derived tool names and input schemas — unchanged. What Graview Cloud does: its connections form offers a Notion connection's data sources as a list — each a data source of the database picked by name and the kind it keeps, with its field-to-property mapping — writing `sources` when there are several, and a "Make the properties it needs" switch (on by default, `create: false` off); it shows `SyncReport.said` in the connection's activity, and draws nothing of its own for the record's line, which the pages face draws.
