An act runs under its caller's sight. The store refused a call naming a record its seat may not see exactly as one naming nothing, but an act's own body read the whole store: a guard that counted holds on a room refused a guest when a hold it could not see was there and let the booking through when there was none, so the guest learned the hold existed — and a page on a served app, which had shown the booking from the guest's view, took it back after the host's second answer. Now an act's body, its condition, its refusal and its sentence read the store as its author is served it (`seenBy`), so a hidden record is to the act what an absent one is: whether it runs, what it refuses with and why, what it writes and what problems it answers with are the same for both, on the tool runtime, `graview serve` and a page alike, and the page's guess is the host's. What it writes still lands on the whole store, and the store's own bookkeeping reads the whole store — a removed record's links and fields, a patch's prior values, a single source's other links, an id already taken — so nothing hidden is lost or overwritten. A call naming a record withheld from its seat whole (for a required field that names one it may not see) is now refused as missing too, before the act runs, and a seat's view's `wouldChange` and `preview` ask as that seat. The sentence "“<act>” could not be done as asked.", which hid an act's refusal worded from a hidden record, is gone: no act can word one now. `graview check`'s `act-reads-hidden-kind` says what the act does instead: run by that role, it judges as though there were none. docs/stability.md §3 no longer lists an act's own logic as what sight cannot hide. The hosted page's first load is 472 B smaller.

Compatibility: ops, stored formats, wire messages, the document format, the compiled format, check finding codes and tool names and schemas are unchanged. What changes is what an act sees when a seat with a sight runs it: an act whose body reads records its author may not see — a TypeScript `apply`, a document act's `allowedWhen`, `refusal`, `sets` or effects — now reads none of them for that author, and so may run where it refused before, or write a count or copy without them. A rule that must hold across records a seat may not see (no double booking) belongs in an invariant, which the store still judges over everything; `graview check` names each act this touches with `act-reads-hidden-kind`. The system, and any seat from whom nothing is kept, is unchanged. `Store#wouldChange` takes the author as an optional second argument.
