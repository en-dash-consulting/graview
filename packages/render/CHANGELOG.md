## 0.1.31

### Patch Changes

- a2f741b: The scene draws on the GPU by default where the browser can, and on the DOM path everywhere else, silently and per page (Roadmap f-gpu-default). `Scene`, the `Shell` and the embed default to `renderer="auto"`: where a page has WebGPU, an element-capture call and a canvas `paint` event (`gpuCapturePresent`, asked without a device), `@graview/render/gpu` is fetched and `attachGpu()` takes the canvas; a page without them — Firefox, Safari, Chromium before element capture — never fetches it and says nothing. A first-frame check falls back to the DOM path for good when no adapter is given, no paint arrives within 1.5 s, a frame is refused, the device is lost, or the middle of the first four frames' work is over three quarters of a frame. A renderer is handed `fallBack()`, `blur` and `scheme` with each picture. `?renderer=gpu|dom` on the address, or `graviewRenderer` set on the page before it loads, decides `auto` for an operator (`rendererAsked`, `chooseRenderer`); `GRAVIEW_RENDERER` does it for every page of a harness run, and the nightly runs every shard on both paths.
  
  The two paths draw one picture: the treatment of a view — its plane style, blur, and fade — is worked out once (`treatmentOf`, `recededOpacity`) and drawn by CSS on one path and the shader on the other, in the page's scheme. The shader no longer mixes toward a fixed ground color or darkens a quad's edges, and it premultiplies the capture's straight alpha once: a light card on the GPU had gray bands down its sides and a pale fringe for a shadow. The accessibility tree is the same on both paths.
  
  On Chromium 158 the browser's own hit-test follows the geometry a view is reported at, without the crash 154 had, so there the canvas says `data-graview-hit="platform"`, the views' content takes the pointer, and hover — a record's ties drawn as the pointer reaches a chip — works on the GPU as on the DOM path; earlier builds keep `PointerRouter`. Geometry is reported a budget a frame until every view is, and a view gone off the canvas is reported there rather than left, invisible, where it was last drawn. A drag or a wheel moves the canvas by a transform, as it moves the DOM path's stage: at altitude the GPU path panned at 20 to 30 frames a second on a quartered processor, re-laying the picture for each notch.
  
  Receded planes are blurred, the focused plane sharp (Roadmap f-plane-blur). On the GPU the blur costs nothing measurable; the DOM path's CSS blur is dropped for the rest of the page if its frames run over a frame and a half while the picture moves. `blur={false}` on the Scene or the Shell turns it off, and so do `prefers-reduced-motion`, `prefers-reduced-transparency` and the reader's Motion setting (`planeBlurOn`). `pnpm verify renderer` holds all of it.
  
  Nothing up front changes on a hosted page; the scene face before it draws is 7.5 KB larger (940,163 bytes), and the embed's budgets are raised in `scripts/budget-raises/`, the capture path itself (about 23 KB) a chunk of its own fetched only where a page can capture.
  
  Compatibility: no surface changes; `Scene` and `Shell` take an optional `blur`, the Shell's `renderer` defaults to `auto` where it was `dom`, `attachRenderer`'s argument gains `blur`, `scheme` and `fallBack`, the frame plan's `PlannedView` takes `depth` and `onPlot`, `PlanOptions` a `scheme`, `ViewDraw` carries `blur` and `FramePlan` `offCanvas`, `PlatformCapabilities.hitTestFollowsGeometry` is now decided, and `@graview/render` gains `gpuCapturePresent`, `rendererAsked`, `chooseRenderer`, `planeBlurOn`, `framesHeld`, `FRAME_BUDGET_MS`, `treatmentOf` and `recededOpacity` (`./gpu`: `FIRST_FRAMES`, `FIRST_PAINT_WAIT_MS`, `FIRST_FRAME_BUDGET_MS`, `Compositor.setBlur`, `Compositor.setScheme`).

## 0.1.30

### Patch Changes

- 8ca608c: The GPU capture path keeps a page of live views under the capture cliff. Chrome Canary 158 respelled HTML-in-Canvas and every capture threw; the canvas now says `content="drawable"` as well as `layoutsubtree`, each view carries `drawable`, and the capture call's destination shape is found on the first call. Capture costs about 0.22 ms a view on that build, so retaking every live view every frame held 42, 21, 10 and 6 fps at 64, 128, 256 and 512 views (median of three runs). A live view is now retaken only when the `paint` event's `changedElements` says it changed, a view off the canvas is neither taken nor drawn, a frame spends at most `CAPTURE_BUDGET` (32) retakes, and a view that keeps changing faster than that is handed to the DOM path and drawn at the same transform under the canvas. The discography holds 417 fps with 512 live views moving every frame, and 125 with the processor at a quarter speed, and `verify-scale` holds it at 256 and 512. `attachGpu()` from `@graview/render/gpu` is the capture path as a Scene's `attachRenderer`; the discography opts in with `?gpu=1`. The uniforms of every view go up in one write, and geometry is reported only for what moved, once a crowd stops.
  
  Compatibility: no surface changes. New on `@graview/render`: `CAPTURE_BUDGET`, `PlanOptions.changed` and `.captureBudget`, `PlannedView.dom`, `FramePlan.deferred` and `.culled`, and a `viewId` on each of `FramePlan.placements`; `planFrame` no longer draws a view wholly off the canvas. ## 0.1.29

### Patch Changes

- 7966f5d: A change says what it was based on, and a link made less than sure is drawn as such until a person keeps it (FR-172). An author said who moved and nothing said why one change was made or how sure its maker was, so a background actor that links and prunes on its own ("Graview, tidying") could not show its reasons or be told apart from a person's work. An op may now carry `lineage: { basis, confidence?, model? }` — given as `ApplyOptions.lineage` to `apply`, `applyAll`, `applyPrimitives` and `undo`, or on an `AppendOp` — checked before anything is kept (a basis of 1–500 characters, a confidence from 0 to 1, a model of 1–120) and kept in the log, on the batch and on the wire. On the op rather than its author, because an author is the same for every change a seat makes and a basis is this change's own. It is said where the change is: "based on “both name 12 Harbor Street” · 60% sure · jev-1" on a record page's history line and in the scene's recent changes, and a record page says what the record was made on. A link whose making op was less than sure, by anyone but a person, is provisional: the scene draws it in short dashes and quieter, its basis on hover and in its acts when it is pressed or long-pressed, and a record page marks it "unsure", the mark saying the basis and offering "Keep this link". Keeping it is `keep-link` (`KEEP_LINK`), an act every store answers to and no list offers, which takes the link away and puts it back as the person's op; any person's op that takes the link away does too, and undoing the keep makes it provisional again. An undo of a change with a lineage keeps it as `undoneLineage`, so a host can read "do not make this again" — a merge (FR-173) as much as a link. `@graview/core/provenance` is the one answer every face and host reads — `linkProvenance(store, edge)`, `isProvisional`, `provisionalLinks`, `provenanceWords` — fetched with what draws a link, so the hosted page's first load stays under its budget. Nothing in the framework decides what to link.
  
  Compatibility: ops gain two optional fields, `lineage` and `undoneLineage`, additive within op format 1: an op without them reads and folds as before, and a build that predates them keeps them on an op it receives. `Batch` gains an optional `lineage`. The wire's `call` and `undo`, on the socket and over `POST /graview/ops`, may carry `lineage`; a change whose lineage is not one is refused as `invalid`, and a server that predates it ignores the field. Every store answers to `keep-link` (`{ kind, from, to }`), which no list of acts — `allMutations`, `permittedMutations`, an agent's tools — offers; an app that declares its own `keep-link` keeps it. Stored formats, the document format, check finding codes and derived tool names and input schemas are unchanged. `capabilities().shipped` gains FR-172. What a host does: a background actor passes `lineage` with each change it makes, reads `linkProvenance` or `provisionalLinks` for what a person has not yet kept, and reads an undo's `undoneLineage` for what a person declined.

  the app was built to make: as a coach the button picks the team; switch to
  analyst and it is refused in the same breath the actions strip refuses it.
  
  The chrome moved into `AgentSeat` in the workbench, where the rest of the
  not-about-the-domain chrome already lives. An app supplies a count, the
  mutation it is really asking for, and the turn itself. `scripts/verify-seat.mjs`
  (`pnpm seat`) holds 17 criteria across the four seats: each states its count,
  each turn changes the graph, each goes quiet afterwards, none throws, and one
  policy narrows the seat as well as the strip.
  
  **The kinds plane became a plane of glyphs.** It took a fifth of the window to
  draw cards covering a tenth of it: 190 by 107 each, holding a name, a number,
  two clamped lines of prose and a row of dots. The comments admit where the dots
  came from — the card "was a name, a number and a great deal of empty
  rectangle", so something was invented to fill it. That is backwards. The
  description moved to the tooltip, the dots became a three-pixel proportion bar
  that says the same thing faster, and the band went from 19% of the height to
  11%. The focus got 84 pixels back.
  
  **The scene can be moved.** Pins have been in the model since the first commit
  — `ViewState.pins`, a round trip through the URL, `layout()` letting a pin beat
  the computed position — with no gesture attached to any of it. Dragging a card
  now sets one. Dragging the ground pans, which needed `ViewState.pan` to exist
  at all; it is view state rather than a camera held to one side, so it is in the
  address, it interpolates, and it comes back when someone opens the link. A move
  is an ADJUSTMENT of the stop you are on rather than a new one, so `useUrlSync`
  replaces instead of pushing and one drag is one history entry rather than
  sixty. The trail gains a "moved ×" crumb, and Escape takes it off before it
  takes anything else off. `scripts/verify-moving.mjs` (`pnpm moving`) holds ten
  criteria, including the one that matters most: a drag is not a click.
  
  Pointer capture is taken when the drag STARTS, not when the pointer goes down.
  Taken on pointer-down it redirects the compatibility mouse events too, so every
  click on an inner target was reported against the host instead — double-clicking
  a task opened its card rather than travelling into it, and `verify-navigation`
  went from 14 criteria to 2.
  
  **Selecting something no longer moves the picture.** Insetting the scene only
  while the strip was showing fixed the collision and bought a worse fault: every
  click reflowed the whole scene, so the thing you clicked slid out from under
  the pointer as its actions appeared. The room is reserved permanently and the
  strip appears inside it. A one- or two-row strip — nearly every selection —
  moves nothing at all; an unusually tall one still pushes rather than covers,
  because being tall is rare and being covered is never right.
  
  **A brand has a third axis, and the apps use all of them.** Palette and
  wordmark were not enough: with `brandFromAccent` deriving everything from one
  hex over a shared base, four apps looked like the same application four times
  in different colours. `Brand.shape` adds a corner radius and a density
  multiplier, emitted as `--graview-radius`, `--graview-pad` and `--graview-gap`,
  so a bid desk can be square and tight and a household planner round and roomy
  without a component knowing whose product it is. The display face now reaches a
  panel's title, which is the largest text on most screens — it had been reaching
  the wordmark and nothing else, because the stylesheet gives it to `h1`–`h4` and
  a panel title is a `strong`.
  
  Each app now declares a real typeface: Figtree for the checklist, Fraunces over
  a rounded sans for the household, a Plex face for the bid desk, a narrow grotesque for the club.
  Three of them had declared `ui-sans-serif` and `ui-serif`, which resolve to the
  same faces everywhere and so were no declaration at all.
  
  `verify-brand.mjs` asserted the literal string "Inter" — a test that passes for
  exactly one brand and fails the moment anyone rebrands, which is the opposite
  of the claim it exists to check. It asks the declaration now: whatever the
  brand put first in its stack is what the page must render in, the face must
  actually have loaded rather than fallen through the stack, the display face
  must reach the headings, and the shape must reach the pixels. Nine criteria,
  up from six.
  
  **Two bugs found by the survey while doing it.** A kind card's title and count
  had ended up inside the proportion bar's conditional, so a kind with no members
  rendered an empty card. And every shrink on the kinds plane is proportional —
  secondary at 0.74, tucked at 0.8 of that, tucked again by how many share a
  parent — so at a glyph-sized band the multiplications landed under the content
  and the cards clipped by four or five pixels. Proportion is right until it
  crosses the floor; there is a floor now.
